## 1. Decision template (economic thinking)

```
1. Who are the decision-makers? (firm, rivals, customers, regulators)
2. What are their objectives?
3. What actions are available?
4. What constraints bind? (cost, capacity, contract, law)
5. What are the trade-offs?
6. What do others do in response? (game theory)
7. What is the equilibrium outcome?
```

**Profit types:** Accounting π = R − AC · Economic π = R − (AC + opportunity cost).

## 3. Demand analysis

**Downward-sloping demand:** higher P → lower Q.

**Elasticity ε:**
- |ε| > 1 elastic — price cuts boost revenue (if costs allow)  
- |ε| < 1 inelastic — price cuts hurt revenue  

**Segmentation link:** different segments → different ε → third-degree discrimination.

---

## 4. Cost & supply (competitive firm)

```
Total cost = FC + VC
MC = d(TC)/dQ
```

Competitive firm supplies where **P ≥ MC** (upward-sloping supply derived from MC).

**Market supply** = horizontal sum of firm supplies.

## 5. Equilibrium comparative statics

| Shock | Short-run effect | Long-run effect |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|
| Demand ↑ | P↑, Q↑ | Entry → may dampen P |
| Supply ↓ | P↑, Q↓ | Exit or new capacity |
| Regulation (tax, quota) | Shift curves | Entry/exit adjusts |

## 6. Profit maximization with market power

```
Profit π = TR − TC
Optimum: MR = MC
```

**MR curve:** below demand when facing downward-sloping demand.

**Lerner index (conceptual):** market power shows as P > MC; higher with lower elasticity.

## 9. Game theory basics

**Players · Actions · Payoffs · Information · Timing**

**Nash equilibrium:** no player wants to unilaterally change strategy.

**Prisoner's dilemma:** individual incentive → collectively suboptimal outcome.

**Commitment / credibility:** threats must be rational when executed.

## 10. Bertrand vs Cournot

| Model | Strategic variable | Homogeneous goods intuition |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Bertrand** | Price | Drives P toward MC |
| **Cournot** | Quantity/capacity | Softer price competition |

**Differentiated products:** Bertrand → partial price matching; less extreme race to bottom.

**Direct vs strategic effects:**

```
Total effect = Direct effect + Strategic effect
```

- **Direct:** impact holding rival strategy fixed  
- **Strategic:** impact after rival's best response  

**Stackelberg:** First mover on capacity — may expand beyond Cournot when strategic effect on rival's output is positive.

**Entry deterrence:** Predatory pricing (P < AVC) · exclusive dealing · bundling/tying · strategic overcapacity.

**First-mover / capacity:** can shift outcomes (Stackelberg-style advantage when credible).

## 11. M&A economics

### Horizontal
- **Market power effect:** ↑ prices, ↓ consumer surplus (watch antitrust)  
- **Efficiency effect:** cost synergies  

**HHI:** Σ(share²). US: highly concentrated >2,500 (2010) or >1,800 (2023); ΔHHI >200 (2010) or >100 (2023) triggers presumption.

### Vertical
- **Double marginalization** reduction (can ↑ total pie)  
- **Hold-up problem:** relationship-specific investment → ex post extraction → underinvestment  
- **Foreclosure risk** if essential input withheld from rivals  
- **Remedy toolkit:** licensing, firewall, no exclusives (Google–ITA pattern)

### Alliance vs integrate
- Alliance: contractual alignment when assets are complementary and risks lower  
- Integrate: when hold-up or quality control failures are severe  

**Principle:** create value by fixing distortions → then divide surplus.

## 12. Strategic ESG (Topic 5)

```
Responsive ESG ──→ Strategic/integrated ESG ──→ Purpose-driven ESG
     (compliance)         (Porter value chain)        (Patagonia)
```

**Inside-out:** Fix negative impacts in operations.  
**Outside-in:** Social change that strengthens competitive advantage.

**Game-theoretic lens:** ESG free-riding prisoner's dilemma — strategic ESG ties investment to differentiation.

## 13. Network economics & platforms (Topic 7)

```
Network value ≈ u · n²/2     (Metcalfe)
Platform (2-sided) ≈ a·m·n     or a·m·n + b·n²
```

**Tipping condition:** n_A·u_A > n_B·u_B → migration accelerates.

**Super-app loop:** Users → payments/data → cross-sell services → higher switching costs.

**Invasion vectors:** Cohort niches · multi-homing · local/geographic · underserved segment · leverage existing user base.

**Antitrust:** Serial "Pac-Man" acquisitions aggregate power across markets.
