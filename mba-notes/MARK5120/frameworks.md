## 2. Five-C framework

Use before STP when the market boundary is unclear.

- **Customers** — needs, behaviors, decision journey  
- **Competitors** — direct/indirect per market-definition level  
- **Company** — capabilities, constraints, strategic assets  
- **Collaborators** — suppliers, channels, complementers  
- **Context** — PESTLE; shocks (e.g. COVID) re-shape categories

## 3. Market definition levels (Session 2)

| Level | Example lens |
|-------|----------------|
| Brand | Direct feature-parity rivals |
| Category | Same job, adjacent form factor |
| Generic | All ways to meet the need (noodles vs food delivery) |

**Errors:** Too narrow → blind to substitutes. Too broad → no strategic clarity.

## 4. STP pipeline

### Step 1 — Market definition  
Bound the competitive arena aligned to objective.

### Step 2 — Segmentation  
Divide into groups that respond differently to marketing tactics.

**Segmentation variable families:**
- Consumer characteristics (geo, demo, psycho, lifestyle)  
- Market history (usage, loyalty, price sensitivity, shopping patterns)  
- **Value-based** (benefits, occasion, motivation) — preferred strategically  

**Outputs:** Segment profile, persona, link to observable traits.

### Step 3 — Targeting  
Choose segment(s): mass · multitarget · niche.

**Attractiveness criteria:** size, growth, profitability, competitive intensity, synergy, competency fit, strategic alignment.

**Evaluation matrix:** Segment attractiveness (Y) × Firm competitive strength (X). Bubble = size; consider growth.

**Tactical targeting:** Map unobservable value segments → observable profiles (avoid shotgun / shot-in-the-dark).

**M-S-D-A-D test:** Measurable, Substantial, Accessible, Differentiable, Actionable.

## 5. Value proposition & positioning (Session 3)

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| Value proposition | Benefits promised to deliver |
| Positioning | Unique, distinctive place in mind vs competitors |

**Positioning statement:**

`For [target], Brand X is the [FOR] that [POD] because [RTB].`

**Frame of reference:** Sets category membership, competition set, consumer expectations.

**POP / POD:** Always relative to FOR.

**Customer value types:** Economic · Functional · Psychological.

**3R principle:** Relevance · Resonance · Realism.

**Perceptual mapping:** Reveals actual vs intended position.

## 6. Product (Session 4)

- Product = tangible operationalization of value proposition.  
- Winning modes: **difference** (horizontal/taste) vs **superiority** (vertical/rankable).  

**Portfolio management lenses:**
- Strategic intent · Customer logic · Product roles · Portfolio balance · Capability fit · Choice discipline  

**BCG:** Stars, Cash cows, Question marks, Dogs — invest/harvest/divest logic.

**Ansoff:** Market penetration · Market development · Product development · Diversification.

**Brand (reading):** Brand equity, resonance pyramid (salience → judgments/feelings → resonance), extensions vs line extensions, co-branding risks.

### Brand meaning management (509-041)

| Concept | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Traditional view | Name/symbol for identification and risk reduction |
| Contemporary view | **Meaning-making asset** — associations in consumer memory shape experience (Pepsi Challenge / Coke brain imaging) |
| Tangible vs intangible | Performance attributes (easy to copy) vs identity/status/lifestyle (hard to copy) |
| Identity vs image | **Intended identity** (firm message) vs **received image** (consumer interpretation) — manage the gap |
| Brand authors | Consumers, media, influencers, culture — not only brand managers |
| Consistency vs relevance | Update aesthetics while preserving core meaning (Betty Crocker portraits) |
| Identity function | "We are what we have" — brands as props for self-expression (Belk) |
| Personality (Aaker) | Sincere · Exciting · Competent · Sophisticated · Rugged |

### Keller Brand Resonance Pyramid

```
                    RESONANCE (loyalty, community, routine)
                   /                                    \
    FUNCTIONAL PATH                          PSYCHOLOGICAL PATH
    (performance → quality judgments)        (imagery → feelings)
                   \                                    /
                    SALIENCE (awareness, recall, top-of-mind)
```

Build **both** paths — functional alone is insufficient for deep loyalty.

### Value innovation / Blue Ocean (Session 4)

| Traditional | Value innovation |
|-------------|------------------|
| Outperform on existing dimensions | Redefine which dimensions matter |
| Feature race → price wars | Uncontested market space |
| "Compete harder" | "Change the game" |

**Four Actions Framework:** Eliminate · Reduce · Raise · Create (strategy canvas).  
Examples: Southwest (eliminate meals/seat assignment, create point-to-point) · Cirque du Soleil · Yellow Tail (simplified wine for non-wine drinkers).

**Portfolio roles:** Gateway · Halo · Growth engine · Cash generator — assign SKUs explicitly before BCG/Ansoff.

## 7. Pricing (Session 5 + EVC reading)

### CORE pricing

| C | Cost — floor, structure, scale |
| O | Objective — profit, demand, competition, brand |
| R | Rivals — actions, position, structure, intent |
| E | Experienced value — WTP ceiling, elasticity, psychology |

### Strategy families

1. Cost-driven (cost-plus, target return)  
2. Value/demand-driven (value-based, dynamic, surge)  
3. Competition-driven (parity, penetration, predatory)  
4. Strategic positioning (skimming, prestige, loss leader)  
5. Relationship (subscription, loyalty, contract)  

### EVC mechanics

1. Compare new vs **reference product** on **lifecycle cost**.  
2. EVC = max WTP; price below EVC for switching incentive.  
3. Different customers → different EVC → **segmentation implication**.  
4. Non-economic barriers (belief, availability, fear) can block adoption despite EVC.  

### WTP research methods

Field experiments · Surveys · Conjoint · EVC analysis.

### Profit drivers (Session 5a)

| Variable | Profit sensitivity (course framing) |
|----------|-------------------------------------|
| Price | ~3.3× — highest leverage |
| Variable cost | ~3.3× |
| Volume | ~2.3× |
| Fixed cost | Lower per-unit impact at scale |

**FC:VC ratio:** High FC (software/media) → scale favors lower incremental price; high VC (commodities) → margin protection critical.

## 8. Communication (Session 6)

**Marketing communication plan blocks:**

| Block | Questions |
|-------|-----------|
| Strategy | Goal focus? Benchmarks? |
| Message | Audience? Message? |
| Tactics | Media? Creative expression? |
| Implementation | Deployment resources? Development process? |
| Control | Performance metrics? Environment scan? |

**AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action — align campaign goal to funnel shift.

### Communication plan — eight components (Session 6)

| Layer | Components |
|-------|------------|
| Strategy | Goal · Audience · Message |
| Tactics | Media · Creative |
| Implementation | Development · Deployment |
| Control | Performance · Environment |

**Benchmarks:** Must be quantified and time-bound (e.g. "unaided awareness 15% → 35% by Q2").

**Message development:** (1) Identify core barrier to purchase → (2) Address barrier in message → (3) Express differentiated value prop (not generic category claim).

### SUCCES creative checklist

| Letter | Principle | Test question |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| S | Simple | One core idea? |
| U | Unexpected | Breaks autopilot? |
| C | Concrete | Visualizable specifics? |
| C | Credible | Proof / social proof? |
| E | Emotional | Makes them care? |
| S | Stories | Narrative arc? |

### Media mix

| Type | Role | Examples |
|------|------|----------|
| Paid | Buy reach, controlled placement | Search, display, TV, social ads |
| Owned | Direct relationship, low marginal cost | Website, email, app, CRM |
| Earned | Credibility, viral potential | Press, WOM, UGC, influencer organic |

**Digital by objective:** Brand keywords (protect share) · generic keywords (category capture) · retargeting (conversion) · email (retention) · content marketing (trust/authority) · platform-specific social formats.

## 9. Distribution & place (Sessions 1, 4)

| Concept | When to use |
|---------|-------------|
| Push strategy | Need channel cooperation to stock; trade promos, margins |
| Pull strategy | Build consumer demand so channel must carry |
| Channel conflict | Direct vs dealer; mass retail vs specialty — align with positioning |
| Trade margin | Stack margins at each tier; channel economics must work for partners |

**Rule:** Distribution follows targeting — specialty for high-service niche; mass only if positioning supports it.

## 10. Consumer psychology (Session 7)

### Dual funnel mapping

| Marketing stage | Psychological stage | Diagnostic question |
|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Attention | Attention | Did they notice? |
| Interest | Perception | Did they interpret favorably? |
| Desire | Attitude | Do they want it? |
| Action | Behavior | Did they buy? |

### Attention levers

- **Sensory salience** — contrast, motion, size (North Face installation)  
- **Novelty / pleasantness** — surprise, humor, ASMR, cuteness  
- **Relevance** — personal name, goal fit, segment-specific (Sunsilk red-hair ad)  

### Perception mechanics

- Constructive — packaging shape/color sets taste expectations  
- Relative — reference price framing; placebo brand effects  

### Motivation drivers

1. **Emotional** — manage feelings (guilt/pride/fear); arousal level affects action speed  
2. **Social** — informational (bestseller lists) · normative (neighbor comparison) · value-expressive (identity signaling)  
3. **Cognitive** — goal pursuit; goal-gradient (progress bars, pre-stamps)  

### CAC context

Digital CAC rising (Alibaba, Meituan, Pinduoduo trend data in course) — optimize for **attention quality** (relevance) not exposure volume.

## 11. Case analysis protocol (exam-style)

### Step 1 — Situation diagnosis

- **Burning platform:** financial pain · existential threat · launch opportunity  
- **5-C audit:** competency must fit recommendation  
- **Value trap:** company-defined value ≠ customer-defined value (Ford Mustang: horsepower vs vibration/sound)  

### Step 2 — Segmentation & targeting

- Small high-margin segment may beat large low-margin segment  
- Benefit-based segmentation over demographics alone  
- Commoditization signal → emotional/social repositioning  

### Step 3 — Economic logic

- B2B/innovation → **EVC** ceiling mandatory  
- **CAC vs LTV** — retention spend for high-LTV segments beats broad awareness for low-value  

### Step 4 — 4Ps execution

Format: "Target [X] by positioning [Y] as [Z]" → product/price/place/promotion with AIDA stage match.

## 12. Marketing arithmetic (Session 1 + HBS note)

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Contribution | Revenue − variable costs (per unit or total) |
| Fixed costs | Unchanged over relevant volume range |
| Break-even | Revenue = total cost; BE volume = FC / unit contribution |
| Profit impact | Contribution − fixed costs (may exclude some corporate overhead) |
| Trade margin | (Selling − purchase) / selling price at each channel tier |

**Scenario analysis:** When adding fixed spend or changing VC, recalc required volume **and** implied market share — ask if competitors will concede share.
