## What strategy is (and is not)

**Strategy = a choice** about the company’s future — not fluff, not everything you do.

Must explain **HOW** you win via **sustainable competitive advantage** (not a fair fight).

### Six strategy questions (Strategy Triangle)

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **WHAT** will we sell? (products/services) |
| 2 | **To WHOM?** (customer segments) |
| 3 | **WHERE?** (markets/geographies) |
| 4 | **WHAT** do we offer that is **better than competitors**? (value proposition) |
| 5 | **HOW** will we deliver it **and make money**? (activity map / operating model) |
| 6 | **WHY** do we bother? (purpose / driving force) |

**Strategy Triangle:** align **product/market choices** (customer view) with **resources, core competences, values, processes** (internal view).

**Strategic thinking loop:** Understanding → Choice → Action → Measurement (continuous, not annual ritual).

**Hedgehog concept (Collins):** Strategy lives at the intersection of (1) deep passion, (2) what you can be **best in the world at**, and (3) what drives your **economic engine**. Use for personal career strategy and for testing whether a business unit has a coherent core.

**Strategy vs operational excellence:** OE = doing things **better** (efficiency). Strategy = doing **different** things or similar things **differently** — requires trade-offs (choosing what **not** to do).

## One-page strategy statement (HBR: objective · scope · advantage)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Objective** | Specific end goal + **time frame** (not mission/values fluff) |
| **Scope** | Boundaries — customers, products, geographies, channels you **will and won’t** serve |
| **Advantage** | **Value proposition** (why target customer buys you) + **activity alignment** (what only you can deliver) |

Requires **trade-offs** — you cannot win on everything.

## Porter’s Five Forces (industry structure → profitability)

| Force | Drivers to examine |
|-------|-------------------|
| Competitive rivalry | Concentration, differentiation, fixed vs variable cost, price vs innovation competition |
| Threat of substitutes | Other ways customer gets the “job” done |
| Buyer power | Price sensitivity, switching costs, concentration, information |
| Supplier power | Differentiation, importance to value prop, concentration |
| Threat of new entrants | Barriers: capital/scale, scarce assets, IP/R&D, distribution access |

Also consider: **regulation**, **complements**.

**Use for:** market entry attractiveness, shifting resources across profit pools, predicting where future profit will migrate.

**Good vs bad competitors:** good competitors expand category and profit pool; bad competitors shrink it via me-too and price wars.

## Customer & competitor analysis (Stage 1a–1c)

**Needs-based segmentation:** Segment by **jobs to be done** (functional + emotional), not demographics alone. Map buying journey and **full product** (core + expected + augmented).

**Basis of competition matrix:** Separate **hygiene factors** (table stakes — must meet minimum) from **basis of competition** (where better performance earns price premium). Don't confuse the two in positioning.

**Competitive asymmetries:** Map structural differences vs rivals — size, cost structure, channel, regulation, data — and design tactics that exploit asymmetries they cannot easily copy.

**Dangerous future competitors:** Look beyond direct rivals to adjacent players who could redefine the industry (e.g. tech platforms entering finance).

## Competitive advantage

**Definition:** How we create and **sustain** advantage vs competitors.

| Type | Examples | Nature |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Asset-based** (snapshot) | Brand, distribution, scale, patents | Can erode as industry evolves |
| **Capability/values-based** (dynamic) | Execution culture, innovation, partner mgmt | Builds and renews assets over time |

Think **magnitude AND rate of change** — are competitors catching up?

**Resources / processes / values** framework for market entry: which advantages **travel** to the new market?

## Strategic fit (Southwest logic)

Before saying yes to an opportunity, ask:

- **What is our strategy?** (Strategy Triangle / scope)  
- **Does this fit?** (core vs peripheral; “we won’t do”)  

**Core** = where the company makes money; **peripheral** = supports core only, not standalone bets.

**Product/market matrix:** map products × segments × geographies × channels → spot synergies and portfolio gaps.

## Portfolio & resource allocation

### BCG Matrix

| Quadrant | Typical stance |
|----------|----------------|
| **Stars** | Invest fully — growth + strength |
| **Cash cows** | Defend, harvest — fund stars |
| **Question marks** | Select 1–2 bets; avoid resource sinks |
| **Dogs** | Minimize; avoid sentimental reinvestment |

Axes: **relative market share** × **market growth**.  
Assumes: limited resources, segments somewhat independent, growth and share drive returns.

### GE Matrix

**Market attractiveness** (size, growth, margins) × **competitive position** — less data-driven, good for **adjacency prioritization** (e.g. segment entry).

**Reality:** allocation is often sticky (last year ±10%) — force a blank-page challenge.

## Market entry & growth

### Core & adjacencies (growth model)

Expand via: new **products**, **customer segments**, **geographies**, **channels**, **value chain** positions — but only where advantage transfers.

### Entry logic

- Treat entry like **focused invasion** — concentration, momentum, **front pin in bowling alley**  
- Assess: Five Forces, profit pool, KSF, disruptive vs sustaining fit  
- **GE matrix** to prioritize adjacencies; gaps → build / partner / JV / acquire  

### Disruptive vs sustaining (Christensen)

| | Sustaining | Disruptive |
|---|------------|------------|
| Targets | Incumbent’s **best** customers | Incumbent’s **less profitable** customers |
| Model | Similar to incumbent | **Different** business model |
| Incumbent response | **Fight** with full resources | May **ignore** until too late |

**Attack options:** stealth · disruptive (flight not fight) · harmless (non-competing).

## Value disciplines (Treacy & Wiersema)

Win by being **best at one**, **good enough** on the other two:

| Discipline | Win through |
|------------|-------------|
| **Operational excellence** | Efficiency, reliability, best price-performance |
| **Customer intimacy** | Deep relationships, tailored solutions |
| **Product leadership** | Innovation, first-mover, superior product |

Misalignment (e.g. claiming product leadership while cutting R&D) breaks strategy.

## Scenarios & seeing the future

Scenarios are **thinking tools**, not predictions.

**Good scenario:** internally consistent, vivid, possible, implies **different actions**, widens view of futures, identifies **trigger metrics**.

**Test:** “Did I make better choices because of this?” — not “Did we predict correctly?”

**Method:** Pick **2 independent, high-impact uncertainties** → cross to **4 consistent scenarios** → for each ask: value chain shifts, capability gaps, white spaces, winners/losers, **no-regrets moves**, **leading indicators**.

**Strategy development process:** Understand today (customer, competitor, company, industry) → See future (trends, scenarios) → Generate alternatives → Evaluate & choose → Action plan.

## Digital future, trends & bets

**Trend matrix (signal vs noise):**

| Bucket | Stance |
|--------|--------|
| **No regrets** | Base case — do regardless of which future unfolds |
| **Big bets** | Invest when signal strengthens; high upside |
| **Long shots** | Contingency / insurance — small bets, option value |

**Observe extreme customers** today to spot mainstream tomorrow. Ask when disruptive tech becomes **“good enough”** and which **new business models** emerge.

**Reinforcing loops (flywheels):** Data loop (usage → data → personalization → value); **network effects** (more users → more value); **liquidity loop** on platforms (more supply ↔ more demand). These drive **winner-take-most** dynamics — plan for scale early where loops matter.

**Digital friction removal:** Distribution, transaction cost, and scale economics change — mid-tier undifferentiated players often **lose** profit; strongest brands and niches gain via demand-pull or global niche access.

**Digital business model patterns:** Freemium · asset-light · omnichannel · sharing economy · long tail · platforms (B2C/B2B/C2C) · everything-as-a-service · micropayments · mass customization.

## Platforms & multisided markets

Three defining features (Evans):

1. Serves **two or more interdependent customer groups**  
2. Value to each side **rises with size of the other side(s)**  
3. Platform provides superior **interaction** between groups  

**Chicken-and-egg:** Solve by subsidizing the harder side (e.g. free for women at dating club; charge merchants on credit cards).

**Pricing:** Direct costs often mislead — **load charges on the side that values participation less** or that you need to attract (marquee users/content).

**Platform playbook:** Start narrow (eBay + Pez), get pricing and kinks right, **then scale** — avoid premature 3G-style over-investment before content/liquidity exists.

**Common models:** **Razor/blade** (loss leader + high-margin consumables) · **Freemium** (free base → premium tier) · **Two-sided liquidity** (Windows, WeChat, marketplaces).

## International growth (Bain matrix)

**Reality check:** ~60% of international initiatives fail; ~33% marginal; ~17% solidly profitable.

**Two axes:** (1) **Economies of scale** — global vs local · (2) **Customer profile** — globally uniform vs locally distinct.

| Quadrant | Strategy |
|----------|----------|
| **Naturally local** | Don't need int'l; if abroad, one market at a time, local customization (supermarkets) |
| **Naturally global** | Think global Day 1; biggest markets; single brand/VP; central ops + local sales (aerospace) |
| **Customer-driven** | Follow global customers; common global VP + local service (ad agencies, B2B) |
| **Compelled by costs** | Modular product — global scale on components + local customization (automotive, entertainment) |

**Four conditions for profitable foreign expansion (Bain cheat sheet):** (1) strong **domestic core**, (2) **repeatable formula**, (3) **portable differentiation**, (4) **favorable host industry structure**.

**Vertical integration / value chain:** Session 5 asks where to play as boundaries shift (e.g. Netflix: content vs distribution vs devices).

## Evaluate alternatives (Stage 3)

For each option assess: **size of prize** (GE/attractiveness), **ability to win**, **partnerships** needed, **implementation barriers**. Use **fast/cheap experiments** for options not ready for full commitment.

**Core competencies (Prahalad & Hamel):** Underlying corporate capabilities (e.g. Honda engines) that are hard to copy, deliver customer value, and unlock **many markets** — build at corporate level, not only SBU silos.

**Poker vs roulette:** Roulette = small bets on everything. **Poker** = wait for **window of opportunity**, then **predatory leap** (big bet). Match bet sizing to conviction and industry clock speed.

**Generate alternatives like IDEO:** Split teams to explore **extreme options on one dimension each** — widens solution space before converging.

## Strategic planning (make it useful)

**Bad process:** annual template theater, junior dusts off last year, defend in review.

**Better process:** review when needed; CEO top-down guidance; debate **issues**; line + staff co-build; success = **new insight and alignment**.

**Critical link:** strategy must appear in **annual budget** and **leaders’ objectives** or it won’t happen.

## Implementing strategy (Stage 4)

**Describe (4a):** **BHAG** + explicit **trade-offs** — what you **stop** doing; **Attack / Defend / Exit** priorities per business.

**Plan action (4b):**

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|----------|
| **Capability gaps** | Close before scaling |
| **Catalytic mechanisms** | Simple enforceable rules that guarantee execution (Collins) |
| **Core values vs operating practices** | Values = timeless, never compromised; practices = adapt to environment — don't confuse when changing |
| **Initiatives & resources** | Balance horizons; **best people on biggest opportunities** — don't spread thin |
| **Alignment** | Budget reflects **new** allocation, not last year's sales targets; cascade to frontline |

**Measure & evolve (4c):** Track **leading indicators** (predictive), not only lagging. Off-track? Diagnose: **execution failure** vs **assumption failure** (experiments wrong) vs **strategic flaw** (VP/advantage obsolete).

**Imitation blockers (strategic fit depth):** 1st order consistency (Vanguard) → 2nd order reinforcing (Neutrogena) → 3rd order optimization (Gap) — **interlocked systems** resist copy.

**Strategic redesign triggers:** Don't wait for annual cycle — pivot when disruptive tech crosses "good enough," asymmetries shift, or scenarios' leading indicators fire.

**Business model vs implementation (Xiameter lesson):** **WHAT** decisions (strategy/model) separate from **HOW** decisions (org, channel conflict, cannibalization, change management).

## Entrepreneurial & innovation mindset

**Start-up ≠ small multinational:** Temporary org creating scalable model under **uncertainty** — needs different governance inside corporates.

**Analysis vs trials/learning:**

| Stable industry | Fast-changing industry |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| 3–5 yr plan, NPV gates | Monthly learning cycles |
| Incremental model change | Many fast cheap experiments |
| Targets realistic | Leading metrics; assumptions challengeable |

**Lean startup loop:** Hypotheses → experiments → measure → learn (Business Model Canvas). Prove **value hypothesis** and **growth hypothesis** before scaling — **premature scaling** is top killer.

**Focused innovation:** Innovate where it extends **value proposition / advantage activities** — not everywhere you merely match competitors.

**Christensen on corporates:** Start-ups should be **patient for growth, impatient for profit**; corporates often reverse this.

## Healthcare / HK subsidiary hooks

- **Five Forces** differs for public tender vs private pay vs global HQ mandate.  
- **Regulatory/compliance** often acts as entry barrier or supplier power (approved vendors).  
- **Subsidiary strategy** = localize Strategy Triangle within HQ scope — explicit **“we won’t do”** list.  
- **Clinical evidence** is often the KSF — marketing (5120) must align with what strategy (5410) can defend.  
- **Disruptive digital health** may bypass incumbents’ asset advantages (distribution, legacy systems).

## 8 questions before a strategy recommendation

1. What is the **objective + timeframe**?  
2. What is **scope** (in/out)?  
3. What is our **advantage** — asset or capability, and is it durable?  
4. What does **Five Forces + value chain** say about industry profit?  
5. Does this fit **core** or distract from core?  
6. **Disruptive or sustaining** — how will incumbents respond?  
7. **Resource implication** — BCG/GE priority vs sticky budget?  
8. **Implementation** — budget, owners, metrics?  

---

## Do / don’t for this agent

**Do:** Name the choice and trade-off; tie recommendation to advantage and industry structure; separate strategy from operations.  
**Don’t:** Fluff words (world-class, synergies); recommend everything; ignore implementation/budget link; conflate mission with strategic objective.
