# MGMT5883 — assignment scaffolding

> Rubrics, checklists, and diagnostic questions. No sample journal. No sample consulting report. No rewritten sentences when a draft is pasted.

The prompts below are the ones printed at the end of the session decks and on the Session 1 assignment slide. Wording is paraphrased.

## Learning journal

**Prompt (Sessions 1–4 close, same three moves).**

1. Summary. Two learning points (insights) from the chosen session. They should be distinct.
2. Personal significance. One of the two, the most valuable, and why it matters to the writer. The slide's example of significance: how the writer might see or approach a pre-existing problem, task, or project differently.
3. Practical application. An example from the writer's own professional experience of how that one insight would be used at work or in life.

**Due:** one week after the session the student chooses.

### Rubric checklist

| Element | Present when |
|---------|----------------|
| Two insights | A reader can point to two different mechanisms, not one mechanism said twice |
| Course grounding | Each insight names a framework, exercise, or case moment from that session |
| Distinction | The two insights sit at different levels (for example individual vs team, or problem-framing vs implementation) or answer different questions |
| Significance | The chosen insight is tied to a real situation the writer already has, with a before/after in how they see it |
| Application | A specific next action, owner, and setting. "Be more creative" does not pass |
| Voice | The prose sounds like the writer's examples and diction. Course terms are used accurately and sparingly |
| Integrity | Facts from class are accurate. No borrowed paragraph. No claim that a teammate's story is the writer's |

### Diagnostic questions (coach asks; student writes)

1. If you deleted the course vocabulary, would a colleague still recognize two different lessons?
2. Which insight did you almost leave out because it complicates the first one?
3. What did you already believe before this session, and where did the session disturb that belief?
4. What will you do on Monday that you would not have done last Monday? Who else has to change for that to work?
5. Which sentence is still a textbook conclusion rather than your situation?

### Session seeds (pick from the session actually attended)

Use these as prompts to remember the class, not as sentences to paste.

| Session | Material the insight can attach to |
|---------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 | A specific obstacle (fixedness, premature problem, one thinking language, single path). One component of Amabile. One discovery behavior, named with the article's word or the lecture's word and the difference flagged. One SCAMPER move tried on a real object |
| 2 | A moment in the gift exercise where the problem statement changed. Nest Fresh: a value, a skill, or a situational constraint — one of them, not the whole decision |
| 3 | Own style vs a teammate's. A time task conflict turned personal, or almost did. Something the HKL exercise showed about unique information. A brainstorming rule the group broke |
| 4 | One Satera dynamic (style, delay, silence, test data). One of: intelligent failure, helping, space, AI, time pressure, skunkworks. The Shin & Grant direction only if the student can say what the class claimed, since the result figure was not recovered as numbers |

### What the coach returns

- Which checklist rows are met.
- One missing distinction or one overclaimed mechanism.
- Two questions that would deepen significance or application.
- Quoted course terms that are used in a way the notes do not support.

The coach does not supply replacement paragraphs.

## Consulting report

**Prompt (Session 1).** Identify two major creativity problems or obstacles at a current or previous employer. For each, give one solution and explain how it would be implemented.

### Rubric checklist

| Element | Present when |
|---------|----------------|
| Two problems | Each is a creativity obstacle in the course sense (novel and useful work is blocked), not a general morale or strategy complaint |
| Evidence | Each problem has observed behavior, a decision, or a metric from the workplace. Sensitivity: strip names, patient data, and confidential numbers before any shareable draft |
| Root vs symptom | The write-up says what the obstacle is doing to expertise, creative-thinking skill, motivation, team process, or the leader's system |
| One solution each | The solution matches the diagnosis. A brainstorming session is not the solution to a moving goal or a relationship conflict |
| Implementation | Who does what, with what resource threshold, on what timeline, and what will be watched to know it worked |
| Trade-off | What the solution costs (speed, control, comfort, budget) and what will be refused |
| Course justification | At least one named mechanism per problem, used at the right level (individual, team, or leader) |
| Boundaries | The writer is not ghost-solving a graded case. Employer facts are the writer's. Recommendations are actionable by someone with the writer's actual authority, or the gap in authority is explicit |

### Diagnostic questions

1. Are these two problems, or one problem in two departments?
2. Which Amabile kill pattern, DNA skill gap, Thompson liability, or Session 4 leader behavior is the best fit — and which nearby pattern did you reject?
3. Does the solution restore intrinsic motivation, or does it add an extrinsic control that the 1998 article warns about?
4. If this is a team problem: where is task conflict, where is relationship conflict, and where is missing unique information?
5. What is the threshold of sufficiency (time, people, access)? What happens below it?
6. What intelligent-failure rule will you use so the first pilot that misses is learned from?
7. Who can veto implementation, and what is your move if they do?
8. Which sentence would embarrass you if the person you are writing about read it? Rewrite that sentence yourself; the coach will not.

### Structure the student can fill (headings only)

```markdown
## Problem 1 — [name the obstacle]
- What is happening (facts):
- Course mechanism:
- Why this is a creativity problem, not only an ops problem:
## Solution 1
- The move:
- Why this mechanism, rather than a neighboring one:
- Implementation (owner, steps, resources, first checkpoint):
- Trade-off and what you will not do:
- How you will know it worked:

## Problem 2 — [a different obstacle]
[same headings]

## What I am unsure about
```

### What the coach returns

- A marked checklist.
- Mismatches between diagnosis and solution.
- One counterargument a skeptical boss would make, left for the student to answer.
- Sensitivity flags (identifiable employer, colleagues, patients, unpublished numbers).

## Participation and cases

Case discussion is part of participation. Preparation is a point of view with a mechanism and a fact, plus the objection to that point of view. It is not a memo written in advance by the coach. Use [cases.md](cases.md).

MBAO rules from Session 1: camera on and the real person in frame; tell faculty and MBAO about an absence; absence above 25% is the program's threshold.

## In-class exercises (practice, not graded prose)

| Exercise | Done when |
|----------|-----------|
| Restaurant assumptions | Three rows: assumption, the challenge, a new idea. At least one idea changes the business, not the adjective |
| Gift-giving 9 steps | A How Might We that names a verb-need and an unexpected insight. Three sketches. One prototype. Feedback in + / ? / questions / ideas |
| Barbara, Joe, Sam | A team goal that is fair to each person's stated aim and that uses each person's strength. An executive structure with a reason |
| Caption brainstorm | Volume first, then a grouped shortlist. Note which Osborn rule broke |
| HKL COO | Individual choice recorded before discussion. Afterward: which unique facts never entered, and whether anyone polled early |
| Satera role play | Decision named, test data used, both styles given a task, relationship content named when it appears |
