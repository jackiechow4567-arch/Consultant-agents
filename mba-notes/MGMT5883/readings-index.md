## 1. The Innovator's DNA

**Claim set used in the notes.** Five discovery skills; associating as the cognitive backbone; the other four as behaviors that feed it. Study scale (25 entrepreneurs, 3,000+ executives, 500 founders/inventors). About 15% of senior executives in the most innovative firms do creative work themselves. About 50% more time on discovery activities. About one-third genetic, two-thirds learned, as the article cites twin research. Questioning, constraint-imposing questions, observation (Cook, Tata, genchi genbutsu), experimenting (including international experience and the ~7% market-performance citation), idea networking vs career networking. Courage to innovate and status-quo bias. Practice drills at the end (daily questions, a day of observation, small experiments, idea lunches).

**Not in this article.** A named four-skill "delivery DNA." The lecture's discovery-and-delivery pairing is Session 3. The lecture's word Combining maps to the article's Associating.

**Where it lives.** [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) §4, [cheat-sheet.md](cheat-sheet.md).

## 2. How to Kill Creativity

**Claim set.** Business creativity = novel and appropriate. Three components, with intrinsic motivation as the near-term lever. Six practices and the kill pattern for each. KEYS (78 items). Team Events Study: Chemical Central vs National Houseware. Physical space is real and is not the highest-leverage practice. Extrinsic motivation can feel controlling; recognition of effort before commercial success is the article's alternative. Failure has information value.

**Where it lives.** [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) §2–3.

## 7. Thompson, *Making the Team*, Chapter 9

**OCR.** Spreads rendered from the photographed course pack and read with Tesseract (`--psm 1`). Pages 1–2 of the PDF are license pages. Content runs from the tail of the previous chapter (p. 200) through Chapter 9 (pp. 201–231). OCR is noisy on footnotes and on some exhibit cells. Prose mechanisms below were checked against the OCR text. Where a number is quoted, the OCR sentence was legible.

**Recovered and used.**

- Creative realism and the four quadrants. Fluency, flexibility, originality (under 5%). Cardboard-box scoring.
- Individuals stronger at divergent thinking; teams stronger at convergent judgment. Separate generation from evaluation.
- Task conflict, debate instructions vs brainstorm instructions, cognitive deviance, Janusian thinking.
- March exploration vs exploitation.
- Osborn's four rules. Rules help; nominal groups still beat face-to-face groups on idea generation and do not beat them on selection. Illusion of productivity.
- Four threats: social loafing, conformity / evaluation apprehension, production blocking, performance matching (downward norm setting).
- Exhibit 9-5 buckets: cognitive-goal, social-organizational, structural-environmental. Paulus's extra rules and the ~40% idea increase. NGT, brainwriting, breaks, Delphi, stepladder, facilitators, diversity, fluid membership, space.
- Cognitive conflict defined as task conflict.
- Tumor/fortress transfer rates (~10%, ~41%, ~85%, ~90%). Inert knowledge. Surface vs deep analogy (~55% vs ~12% in the passage on managerial retrieval).
- Mood citations (positive affect; downsizing; curvilinear evaluative stress).
- EBS advantages and disadvantages. Gore 10% dabble time (mention).
- Closing argument: face-to-face brainstorming's inhibitory factors are pervasive; hybrid methods that use individual generation plus a disciplined group process are the chapter's recommendation.

## 12. IDEO's Culture of Helping

**Status: distilled from OCR** of the image reprint (Amabile, Fisher & Pillemer, R1401C). Mechanisms are in [frameworks.md](frameworks.md) §11.

**Claim set used.** Four keys: leadership conviction, both sides of the helping coin, processes and roles, slack. Verbs in the change section: seek, find, give, receive. Trust and accessibility separate top helpers more than extra competence. One 47-person office; about 89% on someone's top-five list. Status did not wall help off. Senior designers assigned as helpers; design community leaders; reviews required of teams, reviewers not compelled. Slack includes time that could have been billed. Hire for "we"; promote for helpfulness. No helping bonus. The helping tango: the same helper can be a gift or a setback depending on timing and preparation (Ellen / Kathy / Bert are pseudonyms).

**Not in the article, though Session 4 slides teach them.** r = -0.84 for distance and interaction. The word "nooks." An IDEO "love messages" board — the article cites that board as Adam Grant's example of another company.

**OCR gaps.** Page 5 network figure: axes are organizational level by expert status; node size and arrow color are defined in the prose (larger = named more often; one-way toward the helper; mutual ties marked). No tie counts recovered. Page 9 chart: competence, trust, accessibility, low to high, for #1 helper, #5 helper, and a random non-helper. No bar values. The visual read matches the prose and is not quoted as data. Footer noise ignored. Bios in this reprint look updated past 2014.

## How to cite in coaching

| If the student says… | Point them to… |
|----------------------|----------------|
| "The article says combining" | The article says associating. The lecture says combining |
| "Teams are more creative than individuals" | Thompson: not for idea generation. Yes, often, for selection. Session 3: diversity helps if leadership and process hold |
| "More task conflict is better" | Slide 17's curve peaks and bends down. Relationship conflict's line only falls |
| "Satera B says they reconciled" | (B) is the KAI chart |
| "The IDEO article says distance kills help at r = -0.84" or "IDEO has a love wall" | Those are Session 4 slides. The article's levers are trust, access, slack, assigned helpers, and hire/promote for "we." The love wall is Grant's example of another firm |
