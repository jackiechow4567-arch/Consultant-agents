## Recurring decisions I face

1. **Formulary evidence packaging and timing** — What pharmacoeconomic/clinical data to prioritize and refine for the next HA Drug Formulary submission window; how to sequence advisory board input to strengthen the dossier.
2. **PSP structure and expiry management** — Monitor uptake of Base Case (buy 5 get 1) vs Private Sector (buy 2 get 1, expiring end 2026); decide whether/how to adjust terms or messaging as the private-sector deadline approaches.
3. **Promotional material and HCP engagement approvals** — Review quarterly campaign/detail-aid content against UMAO/HKAPI compliance while balancing HCP channel mix (advisory boards, private hematologists, academic centers) to grow private-market share ahead of formulary listing.

## Decision criteria / non-negotiables

| Criterion | Weight / rule | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| HA formulary / public access progress | **High** | Strategic north star for the year; can flex on *sequencing* if evidence isn't ready |
| Patient affordability (PSP) | **High** | Bridges private uptake → formulary case; never let PSP term changes compromise compliance or overpromise pricing |
| Evidence / claims compliance (UMAO, HKAPI, global lexicon) | **Hard constraint** | Always wins — any conflict with revenue, timeline, or HQ preference resolves in favor of compliance |
| Private-market uptake / revenue | **Medium** | Sustains commercial credibility and funding; secondary to formulary progress and compliance |
| Effort / team capacity | **Medium** | Sequence so formulary + compliance work always get priority resourcing over incremental promo activity |
| Strategic fit with HQ global brand | **High** | Local tactics must stay within harmonized global lexicon and brand positioning; no local claim deviation even if commercially tempting |
| Timeline (PSP expiry end 2026, formulary window) | **High** | PSP expiry = forcing function for private-to-public transition messaging; formulary window dictates evidence-packaging deadlines |

**Tie-break order:** Compliance → Formulary/Public Access → HQ Strategic Fit → Affordability → Timeline pressure → Revenue → Team Capacity

**Do:**

- Route every new claim or HCP-facing slide through the global lexicon check before local adaptation or translation.
- Log every reactive HCP scientific exchange (including off-label ET/MF questions) through internal MedInfo/compliance channels, citing current local PI.
- Re-confirm PSP eligibility terms (Base Case vs Private Sector) with market access/finance before quoting any HCP or distributor, especially as the 2026 expiry approaches.
- Cap meal hospitality at HKAPI-defined limits — HK$400/attendee for breakfast/lunch, HK$800/attendee for dinner — when sponsoring HCP events or advisory boards.
- Include full abbreviated prescribing information (approved indication, dosage, contraindications, side effects) and the date of approval on every promotional piece, per HKAPI Section 4.
- Ensure any advisory board or symposium agenda dedicates at least two-thirds of the time to genuine scientific content, documented and distributed in advance.

**Don't:**

- Don't extend or modify Private Sector PSP terms without legal/compliance sign-off, even informally with a key opinion leader.
- Don't reference ET or myelofibrosis data in any HK promotional material or proactive HCP conversation — reactive-only; PV-approved indication stays the boundary.
- Don't submit or update HA Formulary evidence packages without first routing through global Medical/Regulatory review, even under submission-window time pressure.
- Don't offer product-branded promotional items (e.g. branded calendars, mouse pads, sticky notes) to HCPs for a prescription-only medicine like BESREMi — explicitly prohibited under HKAPI Section 6.2.
- Don't provide entertainment (theatre, concerts, sporting events) or lavish hospitality at any sponsored event — HKAPI Section 5.3(h) restricts this to modest, meal-incidental activities only.
- Don't engage HCPs as paid consultants/advisors without a signed written contract, documented legitimate need, and fair-market-value compensation set in advance — undocumented "thank you" fees are a Code violation.

## What strategy is (and is not)

**Strategy = a choice** about the company’s future — not fluff, not everything you do.

Must explain **HOW** you win via **sustainable competitive advantage** (not a fair fight).

### Six strategy questions (Strategy Triangle)

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **WHAT** will we sell? (products/services) |
| 2 | **To WHOM?** (customer segments) |
| 3 | **WHERE?** (markets/geographies) |
| 4 | **WHAT** do we offer that is **better than competitors**? (value proposition) |
| 5 | **HOW** will we deliver it **and make money**? (activity map / operating model) |
| 6 | **WHY** do we bother? (purpose / driving force) |

**Strategy Triangle:** align **product/market choices** (customer view) with **resources, core competences, values, processes** (internal view).

**Strategic thinking loop:** Understanding → Choice → Action → Measurement (continuous, not annual ritual).

**Hedgehog concept (Collins):** Strategy lives at the intersection of (1) deep passion, (2) what you can be **best in the world at**, and (3) what drives your **economic engine**. Use for personal career strategy and for testing whether a business unit has a coherent core.

**Strategy vs operational excellence:** OE = doing things **better** (efficiency). Strategy = doing **different** things or similar things **differently** — requires trade-offs (choosing what **not** to do).

## One-page strategy statement (HBR: objective · scope · advantage)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Objective** | Specific end goal + **time frame** (not mission/values fluff) |
| **Scope** | Boundaries — customers, products, geographies, channels you **will and won’t** serve |
| **Advantage** | **Value proposition** (why target customer buys you) + **activity alignment** (what only you can deliver) |

Requires **trade-offs** — you cannot win on everything.

## Porter’s Five Forces (industry structure → profitability)

| Force | Drivers to examine |
|-------|-------------------|
| Competitive rivalry | Concentration, differentiation, fixed vs variable cost, price vs innovation competition |
| Threat of substitutes | Other ways customer gets the “job” done |
| Buyer power | Price sensitivity, switching costs, concentration, information |
| Supplier power | Differentiation, importance to value prop, concentration |
| Threat of new entrants | Barriers: capital/scale, scarce assets, IP/R&D, distribution access |

Also consider: **regulation**, **complements**.

**Use for:** market entry attractiveness, shifting resources across profit pools, predicting where future profit will migrate.

**Good vs bad competitors:** good competitors expand category and profit pool; bad competitors shrink it via me-too and price wars.

## Customer & competitor analysis (Stage 1a–1c)

**Needs-based segmentation:** Segment by **jobs to be done** (functional + emotional), not demographics alone. Map buying journey and **full product** (core + expected + augmented).

**Basis of competition matrix:** Separate **hygiene factors** (table stakes — must meet minimum) from **basis of competition** (where better performance earns price premium). Don't confuse the two in positioning.

**Competitive asymmetries:** Map structural differences vs rivals — size, cost structure, channel, regulation, data — and design tactics that exploit asymmetries they cannot easily copy.

**Dangerous future competitors:** Look beyond direct rivals to adjacent players who could redefine the industry (e.g. tech platforms entering finance).

## Competitive advantage

**Definition:** How we create and **sustain** advantage vs competitors.

| Type | Examples | Nature |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Asset-based** (snapshot) | Brand, distribution, scale, patents | Can erode as industry evolves |
| **Capability/values-based** (dynamic) | Execution culture, innovation, partner mgmt | Builds and renews assets over time |

Think **magnitude AND rate of change** — are competitors catching up?

**Resources / processes / values** framework for market entry: which advantages **travel** to the new market?

## Strategic fit (Southwest logic)

Before saying yes to an opportunity, ask:

- **What is our strategy?** (Strategy Triangle / scope)  
- **Does this fit?** (core vs peripheral; “we won’t do”)  

**Core** = where the company makes money; **peripheral** = supports core only, not standalone bets.

**Product/market matrix:** map products × segments × geographies × channels → spot synergies and portfolio gaps.

## Portfolio & resource allocation

### BCG Matrix

| Quadrant | Typical stance |
|----------|----------------|
| **Stars** | Invest fully — growth + strength |
| **Cash cows** | Defend, harvest — fund stars |
| **Question marks** | Select 1–2 bets; avoid resource sinks |
| **Dogs** | Minimize; avoid sentimental reinvestment |

Axes: **relative market share** × **market growth**.  
Assumes: limited resources, segments somewhat independent, growth and share drive returns.

### GE Matrix

**Market attractiveness** (size, growth, margins) × **competitive position** — less data-driven, good for **adjacency prioritization** (e.g. segment entry).

**Reality:** allocation is often sticky (last year ±10%) — force a blank-page challenge.

## Market entry & growth

### Core & adjacencies (growth model)

Expand via: new **products**, **customer segments**, **geographies**, **channels**, **value chain** positions — but only where advantage transfers.

### Entry logic

- Treat entry like **focused invasion** — concentration, momentum, **front pin in bowling alley**  
- Assess: Five Forces, profit pool, KSF, disruptive vs sustaining fit  
- **GE matrix** to prioritize adjacencies; gaps → build / partner / JV / acquire  

### Disruptive vs sustaining (Christensen)

| | Sustaining | Disruptive |
|---|------------|------------|
| Targets | Incumbent’s **best** customers | Incumbent’s **less profitable** customers |
| Model | Similar to incumbent | **Different** business model |
| Incumbent response | **Fight** with full resources | May **ignore** until too late |

**Attack options:** stealth · disruptive (flight not fight) · harmless (non-competing).

## Value disciplines (Treacy & Wiersema)

Win by being **best at one**, **good enough** on the other two:

| Discipline | Win through |
|------------|-------------|
| **Operational excellence** | Efficiency, reliability, best price-performance |
| **Customer intimacy** | Deep relationships, tailored solutions |
| **Product leadership** | Innovation, first-mover, superior product |

Misalignment (e.g. claiming product leadership while cutting R&D) breaks strategy.

## Scenarios & seeing the future

Scenarios are **thinking tools**, not predictions.

**Good scenario:** internally consistent, vivid, possible, implies **different actions**, widens view of futures, identifies **trigger metrics**.

**Test:** “Did I make better choices because of this?” — not “Did we predict correctly?”

**Method:** Pick **2 independent, high-impact uncertainties** → cross to **4 consistent scenarios** → for each ask: value chain shifts, capability gaps, white spaces, winners/losers, **no-regrets moves**, **leading indicators**.

**Strategy development process:** Understand today (customer, competitor, company, industry) → See future (trends, scenarios) → Generate alternatives → Evaluate & choose → Action plan.

## Digital future, trends & bets

**Trend matrix (signal vs noise):**

| Bucket | Stance |
|--------|--------|
| **No regrets** | Base case — do regardless of which future unfolds |
| **Big bets** | Invest when signal strengthens; high upside |
| **Long shots** | Contingency / insurance — small bets, option value |

**Observe extreme customers** today to spot mainstream tomorrow. Ask when disruptive tech becomes **“good enough”** and which **new business models** emerge.

**Reinforcing loops (flywheels):** Data loop (usage → data → personalization → value); **network effects** (more users → more value); **liquidity loop** on platforms (more supply ↔ more demand). These drive **winner-take-most** dynamics — plan for scale early where loops matter.

**Digital friction removal:** Distribution, transaction cost, and scale economics change — mid-tier undifferentiated players often **lose** profit; strongest brands and niches gain via demand-pull or global niche access.

**Digital business model patterns:** Freemium · asset-light · omnichannel · sharing economy · long tail · platforms (B2C/B2B/C2C) · everything-as-a-service · micropayments · mass customization.

## Platforms & multisided markets

Three defining features (Evans):

1. Serves **two or more interdependent customer groups**  
2. Value to each side **rises with size of the other side(s)**  
3. Platform provides superior **interaction** between groups  

**Chicken-and-egg:** Solve by subsidizing the harder side (e.g. free for women at dating club; charge merchants on credit cards).

**Pricing:** Direct costs often mislead — **load charges on the side that values participation less** or that you need to attract (marquee users/content).

**Platform playbook:** Start narrow (eBay + Pez), get pricing and kinks right, **then scale** — avoid premature 3G-style over-investment before content/liquidity exists.

**Common models:** **Razor/blade** (loss leader + high-margin consumables) · **Freemium** (free base → premium tier) · **Two-sided liquidity** (Windows, WeChat, marketplaces).

## International growth (Bain matrix)

**Reality check:** ~60% of international initiatives fail; ~33% marginal; ~17% solidly profitable.

**Two axes:** (1) **Economies of scale** — global vs local · (2) **Customer profile** — globally uniform vs locally distinct.

| Quadrant | Strategy |
|----------|----------|
| **Naturally local** | Don't need int'l; if abroad, one market at a time, local customization (supermarkets) |
| **Naturally global** | Think global Day 1; biggest markets; single brand/VP; central ops + local sales (aerospace) |
| **Customer-driven** | Follow global customers; common global VP + local service (ad agencies, B2B) |
| **Compelled by costs** | Modular product — global scale on components + local customization (automotive, entertainment) |

**Four conditions for profitable foreign expansion (Bain cheat sheet):** (1) strong **domestic core**, (2) **repeatable formula**, (3) **portable differentiation**, (4) **favorable host industry structure**.

**Vertical integration / value chain:** Session 5 asks where to play as boundaries shift (e.g. Netflix: content vs distribution vs devices).

## Evaluate alternatives (Stage 3)

For each option assess: **size of prize** (GE/attractiveness), **ability to win**, **partnerships** needed, **implementation barriers**. Use **fast/cheap experiments** for options not ready for full commitment.

**Core competencies (Prahalad & Hamel):** Underlying corporate capabilities (e.g. Honda engines) that are hard to copy, deliver customer value, and unlock **many markets** — build at corporate level, not only SBU silos.

**Poker vs roulette:** Roulette = small bets on everything. **Poker** = wait for **window of opportunity**, then **predatory leap** (big bet). Match bet sizing to conviction and industry clock speed.

**Generate alternatives like IDEO:** Split teams to explore **extreme options on one dimension each** — widens solution space before converging.

## Strategic planning (make it useful)

**Bad process:** annual template theater, junior dusts off last year, defend in review.

**Better process:** review when needed; CEO top-down guidance; debate **issues**; line + staff co-build; success = **new insight and alignment**.

**Critical link:** strategy must appear in **annual budget** and **leaders’ objectives** or it won’t happen.

---

## Implementing strategy (Stage 4)

**Describe (4a):** **BHAG** + explicit **trade-offs** — what you **stop** doing; **Attack / Defend / Exit** priorities per business.

**Plan action (4b):**

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|----------|
| **Capability gaps** | Close before scaling |
| **Catalytic mechanisms** | Simple enforceable rules that guarantee execution (Collins) |
| **Core values vs operating practices** | Values = timeless, never compromised; practices = adapt to environment — don't confuse when changing |
| **Initiatives & resources** | Balance horizons; **best people on biggest opportunities** — don't spread thin |
| **Alignment** | Budget reflects **new** allocation, not last year's sales targets; cascade to frontline |

**Measure & evolve (4c):** Track **leading indicators** (predictive), not only lagging. Off-track? Diagnose: **execution failure** vs **assumption failure** (experiments wrong) vs **strategic flaw** (VP/advantage obsolete).

**Imitation blockers (strategic fit depth):** 1st order consistency (Vanguard) → 2nd order reinforcing (Neutrogena) → 3rd order optimization (Gap) — **interlocked systems** resist copy.

**Strategic redesign triggers:** Don't wait for annual cycle — pivot when disruptive tech crosses "good enough," asymmetries shift, or scenarios' leading indicators fire.

**Business model vs implementation (Xiameter lesson):** **WHAT** decisions (strategy/model) separate from **HOW** decisions (org, channel conflict, cannibalization, change management).

## Entrepreneurial & innovation mindset

**Start-up ≠ small multinational:** Temporary org creating scalable model under **uncertainty** — needs different governance inside corporates.

**Analysis vs trials/learning:**

| Stable industry | Fast-changing industry |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| 3–5 yr plan, NPV gates | Monthly learning cycles |
| Incremental model change | Many fast cheap experiments |
| Targets realistic | Leading metrics; assumptions challengeable |

**Lean startup loop:** Hypotheses → experiments → measure → learn (Business Model Canvas). Prove **value hypothesis** and **growth hypothesis** before scaling — **premature scaling** is top killer.

**Focused innovation:** Innovate where it extends **value proposition / advantage activities** — not everywhere you merely match competitors.

**Christensen on corporates:** Start-ups should be **patient for growth, impatient for profit**; corporates often reverse this.

## Pricing (Session 5 + EVC)

### CORE framework

| Letter | Lens |
|--------|------|
| **C**ost | Floor — FC:VC ratio, scale, cost vs rivals |
| **O**bjective | Profit / demand / competition / brand signal |
| **R**ivals | Action, position, market structure, strategic intent |
| **E**xperienced value | Ceiling — WTP, elasticity, psychology, fairness |

**Rule:** Cost sets floor; **customer experienced value sets ceiling**. Compete on perceived value, not price cuts alone.

**Profit driver sensitivity:** 1% price change often beats equivalent volume or VC change (~3.3× impact factor in course framing). Price is the highest-leverage profit lever — justify with CORE, don't cut reflexively.

**Skimming vs penetration:** Skimming = high price, lower volume (distinctive/premium). Penetration = low price, drive adoption/habit. Link choice to objective + EVC ceiling.

### Common strategy buckets

Cost-driven · Value/demand-driven · Competition-driven · Strategic positioning · Relationship/retention.

### EVC (Economic Value to Customer)

- **Max price** customer *should* pay vs reference product (lifecycle / cost of ownership)  
- **Relative** — differs by customer usage pattern → **segments + targeting**  
- Charge **below** EVC to give switching incentive  
- Economic benefit alone may fail — need RTB on durability, availability, non-economic value  

### Elasticity

`|ΔQ/Q| / |ΔP/P|` — inelastic <1 · elastic >1.  
Sensitivity drivers: substitutes, comparability, % of wallet, switching cost, stockpiling.

## Consumer psychology (Session 7)

### Attention (gateway)

## Demand (Topic 1)

**Demand** comes from consumer preferences and willingness to pay.

**Key ideas:**
- Consumers maximize utility subject to budget → **demand curve** slopes down  
- **Indifference curves:** trade-offs between product attributes (quality vs features)  
- Shift factors: income, tastes, substitutes/complements, expectations, regulation  

**Elasticity:**

```
Price elasticity of demand = (% Δ quantity) / (% Δ price)
```

| |Elastic| |Inelastic|
|---|--------|-----------|
| |ε| > 1 | |ε| < 1 |
| Small price change → large Q change | Large price change needed to move Q |

**Pricing rule of thumb (market power):** lower elasticity → can charge **higher markup**.

**Case pattern (citizenM):** radical offer design shifts who buys and at what WTP.

---

## Supply & competitive markets (Topic 2)

**Competitive market features:** many buyers/sellers, homogeneous product, good information, **easy entry/exit**.

**Firms are price takers** — take market price as given; choose **quantity**.

**Cost types:**
| Cost | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Fixed (FC) | Does not vary with output (rent, licenses) |
| Variable (VC) | Varies with output (materials, hourly labor) |
| Marginal (MC) | Cost of one more unit |

**Short-run vs long-run:**
- **Short-run:** some costs fixed → supply less flexible  
- **Long-run:** entry/exit, capacity adjusts → **more elastic** supply and often demand  
- Long-run supply can slope up (typical), flat, or down (strong economies of scale)  

**Equilibrium:** price adjusts until quantity demanded = quantity supplied.

**Policy/shock analysis:** trace shifts in supply or demand curves → new equilibrium price/quantity.

---

## Market power & pricing (Topic 3)

Applies to **monopoly and oligopoly** firms with pricing discretion.

**Core rule:** For a given demand curve, choose **price OR quantity**, not both independently.

**Profit maximization:** at optimal Q*, **MR = MC**  
**Revenue maximization:** produce where **MR = 0**

**Example:** P = 100 − Q, MC = 10, FC = 500 → Q* = 45, P = 55.

**Trade-off:** high margin / low volume vs low margin / high volume.

### Price discrimination

Charge different prices when **same product** could be sold at different prices to different segments.

| Type | Idea | Risk |
|------|------|------|
| **Direct (3rd degree)** | Observable segments with different elasticities | Fairness backlash, arbitrage |
| **Indirect / versioning** | Unobservable types self-select via product versions | Cannibalization |

**Rule:** charge **higher price** to **less elastic** segments.

**Tools:** damaged goods, feature stripping, tiers (Tinder, airlines yield management).

**Other pricing:**
- **Two-part tariff:** fixed fee F + per-unit price p (clubs, razors/blades)  
- **Bundled discount:** package products to extract surplus  
- **Decoy effect:** asymmetrically dominated option steers choice  
- **Endowment effect:** longer possession → higher perceived value (gym memberships, BNPL)

**Markup intuition:** with market power, **(P − MC) / P** rises as demand becomes **less elastic**.

### Fighting brand (Saurer)

When low-end entrants threaten your high-end share:
- Design a **basic version** strong on raw performance but **fewer value-added features**  
- Attracts low-end buyers, **minimizes cannibalization** of premium SKU  
- Move **proactively** before low-end rivals become full threats

## Oligopoly & game theory (Topic 4)

**Game theory:** payoffs depend on **your action + others' actions** → must **anticipate reactions**.

**Nash equilibrium:** each player's strategy is a **best response** to others' strategies — no unilateral incentive to deviate.

**Prisoner's dilemma (pricing):** individually rational **low price / low price** beats collusive **high / high** — industry profit destroyed unless coordination sustains.

### Bertrand (price competition)
- Firms set **prices**; with homogeneous goods → fierce undercutting → **P ≈ MC**  
- With **differentiated** products → partial price matching, softer competition  

### Cournot (capacity/quantity competition)
- Firms choose **capacity/quantity**; market clears at price from total output  
- Each firm's optimal quantity depends on **rival's capacity**  
- Less destructive than Bertrand when products differentiated or capacity constrained  

**When firms compete in price vs capacity** depends on industry institutions, capacity adjustability, differentiation.

**Direct vs strategic effects:** Any move has (1) **direct effect** on own profit holding rivals fixed, and (2) **strategic effect** from rival's best response. **Total effect** = sum — a price cut can fail if strategic effect dominates (Bertrand).

**Stackelberg (capacity):** First mover expands capacity anticipating rival shrinks — can beat Cournot output when strategic effect is **positive** on capacity.

**Strategic commitment:** Must be **credible** (visible, costly/irreversible). **Aggressive** vs **soft** depends on substitutes vs complements in reaction curves.

**Tacit collusion:** Repeated games — mimic rival's prior action (e.g. high price after high price); fragile without enforcement.

**Entry deterrence tools:**
- **Predatory pricing:** P < AVC to drive rival out, then recoup — needs market power + recoupment (antitrust scrutiny)  
- **Exclusive dealing** with suppliers/distributors  
- **Bundling/tying** so entrant cannot compete with standalone product  
- **Strategic overcapacity** (Dixit-style) — Amazon fulfillment, Maersk ships, 7-Eleven density  

**Universal Rental Car simulation lesson:** dynamic pricing under demand uncertainty — test elasticity, avoid destructive price wars.

## M&A & strategic alliances (Topics 5–6)

### Horizontal M&A
Creates value via:
- **Market power** (pricing, route/share — airline merger evidence)  
- **Efficiency / synergy** (cost savings)  

**Antitrust lens:** will merged entity raise prices? Which segments/routes gain power?

### HHI (Herfindahl–Hirschman Index)

```
HHI = Σ (market share %)²   → max 10,000 (monopoly)
```

| US DOJ/FTC (2010) | Threshold |
|-------------------|-----------|
| Moderately concentrated | HHI 1,500–2,500 |
| Highly concentrated | HHI > 2,500 |
| Presumptive harm | ΔHHI > **200** in highly concentrated market |

**2023 revision:** "highly concentrated" from **1,800**; presumptive harm if ΔHHI > **100** (especially post-merger share ≥ **30%**).

**HK / EU:** use same logic — concentration + ΔHHI + quality-investment concerns (e.g. Staples/Office Depot blocked when market structure changed).

**Empirical M&A note:** Acquired-firm shareholders often gain; **acquiring-firm** shareholders often flat/negative unless disciplined strategy — **overpaying premium** destroys value.

### Vertical integration & alliances
**Value chain distortions** (double marginalization, hold-up, misaligned incentives) can destroy value.

**Hold-up problem:** Relationship-specific investment → partner can **extract surplus** ex post → underinvestment ex ante.

**Double marginalization:** Independent upstream + downstream each mark up → joint profit below integrated optimum.

**Vertical integration** can align incentives but may **foreclose** rivals' access to essential inputs (Google–ITA flight search: incentive to raise rivals' ITA input cost post-merger).

**Remedy pattern (Google–ITA):** Continue licensing on commercial terms · maintain R&D · **internal firewall** · no exclusive airline data contracts.

**Strategic alliance:** fix distortions first (**value-maximization principle**), **then** contract to split surplus — not always full merger.

**Opposite incentives:** horizontal merger ↑ price incentive; vertical integration changes upstream/downstream pricing differently.

**Recent vertical cases:** Microsoft–Activision (content foreclosure concern) · pharma supply-chain quality hold-ups (DEG cough syrup, contaminated eye drops — cost minimization vs quality).

## Strategic ESG & circular economy (Topic 5)

**CSR vs ESG:** CSR = broad social responsibility; **ESG** = measurable Environmental, Social, Governance KPIs.

**Prisoner's dilemma in ESG:** If rivals don't invest, you gain by free-riding; if all invest, industry may be better off — **strategic ESG** breaks this by tying to competitive advantage.

**ESG tiers (Porter/Kramer):**

| Tier | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Responsive | Generic compliance / PR |
| Strategic | ESG integrated with **core value chain** and advantage |
| Purpose-driven | ESG as primary goal (Patagonia) |

**Inside-out:** Reduce negative impacts in operations (emissions, labor, supply chain).  
**Outside-in:** Social change that **strengthens** advantage (e.g. farmer productivity, electrification standards that favor your product).

**Patagonia pattern:** "Don't buy our products" — durability/repair aligns brand with strategic ESG; circular economy extends product life.

## Network economics & platform competition (Topic 7)

**Network value laws:** Sarnoff V ∝ n · Metcalfe V ∝ n² · Reed V ∝ 2ⁿ — course emphasizes **Metcalfe**: value ≈ **u·n²/2** (u = value per connection).

**Tipping:** Users leave network B for A when **n_A·u_A > n_B·u_B**. Once migration starts, gap widens → **winner-take-all**.

**Duopoly instability:** Two equal networks each worth ~¼ of one monopoly network → each willing to invest heavily to topple rival.

**Two-sided platform:**

| Model | Value function | Example |
|-------|----------------|---------|
| Marketplace | V = **a·m·n** (vendors m, shoppers n) | Alibaba e-commerce |
| Social + commerce | V = **a·m·n + b·n²** | WeChat |

**Compete beyond scale:** Add services → higher **u** and **switching costs**. **Super-app:** leverage user base + payments/data across verticals (Grab, Gojek, Meituan).

**Data flywheel:** More services → more data → better personalization/AI → more usage.

**Product → platform:** Adobe (Creative Cloud subscription + Exchange marketplace); Marriott (Homes & Villas + Bonvoy); Flipkart (bookseller → marketplace).

**Why super-apps flourished in Asia:** Mobile-first leapfrogging, underbanked → mobile payments, fewer legacy PC-era silos, higher digital adoption — vs West's GDPR/privacy friction and pre-smartphone specialist apps.

**Balancing sides:** Uber (drivers/passengers), Meituan (merchants/delivery), dating apps — subsidize constrained side (Bumble: women-first to fix gender skew).

**Platform entry / invasion** (when incumbent looks unbeatable):

| Vulnerability | Tactic |
|---------------|--------|
| Age/cohort split | Target teens (Snapchat, TikTok vs Facebook) |
| **Multi-homing** | Users try new app while keeping incumbent — incumbents may "pick one from two" |
| Local/geographic | City-by-city (Uber vs global Airbnb) |
| Underserved segment | Pinduoduo — lower-tier, price-sensitive |
| Own user base | Cross-sell from existing network (Alibaba eWTP: China buyers attract foreign vendors) |

**Antitrust on platforms:** "Pac-Man" serial acquisitions — many small deals aggregate market power (FTC concern).

**Cases:** Meituan super-app · Shein ultra-fast fashion/digital supply chain · Grab/Gojek path · Bumble platform design.

## Healthcare / HK subsidiary caveats

- **Inelastic segments** (urgent care, sole-supplier devices) vs **elastic** (commoditized consumables).  
- **Tender markets:** Bertrand-like price pressure; watch race to MC.  
- **Fighting brand / versioning:** economy line vs premium — manage cannibalization and brand (5120 positioning).  
- **Vertical integration:** distributor/hospital GPO hold-up — alliance vs integrate (Topic 6).  
- **M&A:** horizontal concentration triggers competition scrutiny (Fong/HK Comp Commission lens).

## 8 questions before a pricing/competitive move

1. Market structure — competition, oligopoly, or local monopoly?  
2. Own and cross **price elasticity**?  
3. Fixed vs marginal cost profile — room to cut?  
4. Will rivals match price (**Bertrand**) or adjust capacity (**Cournot**)?  
5. Is discrimination/versioning feasible and fair?  
6. Cannibalization risk on premium line?  
7. Short-run vs long-run entry/exit response?  
8. Antitrust / contract constraints?  

---

## Do / don't

**Do:** State assumptions; separate short-run vs long-run; predict **rival reaction**.  
**Don't:** Assume unlimited pricing power in competitive markets; recommend price cuts without elasticity + game logic; ignore implementation of collusion (often unstable).
