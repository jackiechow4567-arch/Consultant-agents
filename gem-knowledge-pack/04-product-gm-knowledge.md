- Maintain **visible, unannounced-inspection-ready** trial documentation (GCP/BIMO) at all times — not only before scheduled audits

**Don't:**

- Don't approve commercial shortcuts the PM flags as **UMAO/HKAPI risk**, even to hit a monthly stretch target
- Don't starve trial **CAPA or inspection-readiness** work to protect a revenue number
- Don't negotiate HA Formulary terms or **pricing commitments** without HQ sign-off, even under local competitive pressure
- Don't reallocate **PSP** budget to sales incentives without transparent tracking back to HQ

## Why this matters for the BESREMi PM

- **BESREMi is already registered and launched in HK (Oct 2025).** This library is for *future* registration events — not for re-arguing the original launch.
- **New indication / posology / population (e.g. ET or MF)** = typically **NDA-2B** and needs non-clinical and/or clinical data. That remains **investigational / reactive-only** in HK until locally registered. Do **not** treat this file as permission to plan proactive ET/MF promotion.
- **Line extensions** (new strength, dose form, route, combination, container that may change safety/efficacy) = **NDA-2**; administrative / non-impact changes = **NDA-3**.
- **“1+” mechanism** (from 31 Mar 2026, under the **abridged evaluation** route) is the HQ-relevant option when a product has an orphan / breakthrough / priority (or equivalent) approval in a listed reference market **and** Chinese/Asian or local clinical data. Use for pipeline / next-asset access planning, not for on-label PV claims.
- **RA / Drug Office owns the dossier.** PM uses this file for **timeline, access, PSP/formulary sequencing, and HQ narrative** — not to invent Module 3–5 content.

**Tie-back to role memory:** Compliance → Formulary/public access → HQ strategic fit. Registration path is a hard gate for any new claim or indication.

## Who applies (imported product)

For a product manufactured **outside** Hong Kong, the applicant is the **licensed wholesale dealer who imports** it, **or** the HK branch / subsidiary / representative / agent / distributor of the overseas manufacturer.

- Submit via **PRS 2.0** (`www.drugoffice.gov.hk/prs2-ext/client_authentication.jsp`).
- PDFs must be flattened, text-searchable, not encrypted; include ToC, hyperlinks, bookmarks.
- **Application fee:** HK$1,100 (at time of guidance). **Registration fee on approval:** HK$1,370 per product.
- Screening completeness window: outstanding documents within **60 calendar days** or the NDA is **refused for filing** (a new application can be submitted).
- Enquiries: Drug Evaluation and Pharmacovigilance Division, Drug Office — Tel **3974 4175**; Pre-NDA email **pharmgeneral@dh.gov.hk**.

## NDA categories (initial registration)

| Category | Meaning | PM examples |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **NDA-1** | New chemical or biological entity not previously registered in HK | First HK registration of a new asset |
| **NDA-2** | Registered entity, but needs non-clinical and/or clinical data | **2B new indication / dosage / population**; 2C new strength; 2D new dose form; 2E new route; 2H new combination; 2A new salt/ester/isomer if it changes S/E; 2F composition change that may affect S/E; 2G container/presentation that may affect S/E |
| **NDA-3** | Subsequent registration of an already-registered NDA-1/2 product **without** new non-clinical/clinical data | 3A non-impact excipient (e.g. colourant); 3B new manufacturer on label; 3C pack/presentation that does **not** change S/E; 3E new trade name |

**Not this NDA guidance:** generic/reference-product applications that only need bioequivalence; biosimilars; biologicals that do not differ significantly in S/E — those have separate PPB notes.

**Same vs separate applications**

- Non-injectable products that differ **only** in pack size: one application (or a change-of-particulars filing).
- **Separate** applications: different strengths; different dose forms; more than one presentation for the same pack size (unless justified); **injectables with different container volumes**.

## Evaluation routes

| Route | When eligible | Extra PM/RA implication |
|-------|---------------|-------------------------|
| **Verification** | Approved in **two or more** listed reference countries/places (ATP: EU approvals must be from **EMA**) | CTD Modules 2, 3, 5 (Module 4 if needed); PI comparison vs reference markets; at least one listed-market PI to support the HK insert |
| **Abridged** (includes **“1+”** from 31 Mar 2026) | **Either** (i) local unmet need for PHE / specified communicable-disease / AMR use **and** promulgation by WHO or equivalent; **or** (ii) **“1+”**: orphan / breakthrough / priority (or equivalent prominent clinical benefit) **and** currently on market in a listed country **and** local **or** Chinese/Asian clinical data (ICH E5 ethnic-factors logic) | Cover letter + eligibility evidence; **local expert** S/E assessment (fellowship or equivalent, **≥5 years** in-field); for “1+”: unredacted reference-authority assessment reports, post-registration development plan. Ineligible abridged filings may be refused at screening — verification remains open |
| **Primary** | **Not** approved in any listed country | **Pre-NDA meeting is mandatory** before filing. Phase 1 of primary evaluation: **NDA-2 or NDA-3 of chemical entities only**. Incomplete CTD may be refused; abridged/verification still available if eligibility appears later |

## “1+” Pre-NDA meetings (Mar 2026)

From 31 Mar 2026, “1+” sits under **abridged evaluation**. Meetings are optional for abridged “1+” (unlike **mandatory** pre-NDA for **primary** evaluation).

| Type | Who | Length | Purpose |
|------|-----|--------|---------|
| **Company-focused** | **First-time** “1+” applicants; product approved **or under review** in a listed country | **45 min** (DO intro 10 / company 10 / Q&A 25) | Process briefing — **not** advice on generating a new data package |
| **Product-specific** | Generally **NDA-1** under “1+” | **60 min** (company 10 / DO 50) | Dossier/questions on **existing** studies or the proposed package. Data **evaluation** only happens after NDA filing |

**Mechanics**

- Watch the briefing seminar/video **before** requesting.
- Request via PRS 2.0 with **FORM A**, **≥6 weeks** before proposed dates; DO responds within **4 weeks**.
- Package: FORM A (questions), **≤30** slides, product overview (Annex 1), proposed SmPC/PI. **No changes** after submit; reschedule generally **not** accepted.
- **One** company-focused meeting per first-time applicant; **one** product-specific meeting per NDA-1 under “1+”.
- No recording. No promotion. Requester minutes are **not** acknowledged by Drug Office.
- Incomplete package → meeting declined; a new request can be filed.

## Stop-clock (pilot, “1+” evaluation)

Clock **runs** while Drug Office / Core Team / Committee evaluate; **stops** while the applicant prepares clarifications.

| Clock | Limit / target |
|-------|----------------|
| DO **first response** | Targeted within **30 working days** |
| **Cumulative evaluation** (includes Committee decision) | **150 working days** for “1+”; **100 working days** if **Hospital Authority recommends priority review** |
| **Applicant response** (excluded from evaluation time) | Capped at **120 calendar days** cumulative. At the cap, the file goes to the Committee even if S/E/Q are not fully closed |
| Core Team + Committee transaction | Targeted **30–45 working days** (inside the evaluation clock) |
| GMP inspection / inspectorate follow-up | **Not** inside the 100/150 WD target |

**PM translation:** a “1+” promise of ~150 WD is **Drug Office time only**. Applicant-clock + GMP can stretch wall-clock. HA priority review is the lever that shortens the target to 100 WD — coordinate with market access, do not claim it unilaterally.

## Pharmacovigilance and post-registration (commercial impact)

**During evaluation**

- Overseas regulatory actions (withdrawal, refusal, suspension, clinical hold, safety alert, indication restriction, recall, etc.): report within **72 hours**.
- New information that may **significantly change benefit/risk** (e.g. study stop): report.

**After registration**

- Report **all serious ADRs in Hong Kong**; primary/abridged also **unexpected** local ADRs.
- Implement the proposed **RMP** (abridged: keep aligned to global RMP when it changes; primary: update when B/R or a PV milestone changes).
- Submit **final clinical study reports** when they go to other regulators, plus conclusion + follow-up actions; overseas actions from those studies: **≤72 hours**.
- **PBRER** (or equivalent): every **6 months for 2 years**, then **annually for 3 years** (primary/abridged: every 6 months — confirm current DO instruction).
- Sales control: NCE registration generally waits for **legislative amendment** of the Pharmacy and Poisons Regulations. From **June 2018**, amendment can start once an NDA is **accepted for evaluation** (or listed in a public medical assistance programme), unless the Committee must first advise on indication/dose-dependent control. Orphan/breakthrough/priority products may start this on **first submission** (case by case).

**PM Do/Don’t**

- **Do** treat overseas label cuts, recalls, or study stops as a **72-hour RA + PV + PM** event — freeze new promo claims until RA confirms the HK PI.
- **Do** sequence HA formulary / PSP messaging **behind** the registered PI; a new NDA-2B indication is not a commercial campaign until the certificate exists.
- **Don’t** quote “150 working days to listing” as a launch date.
- **Don’t** use Pre-NDA discussion as evidence of approvability in HCP materials.
- **Don’t** overlook HK patent risk: PPB **does not** consider patents, but Cap. 514 infringement remains the applicant’s problem.

# HK Employment Ordinance — GM cheat sheet

> Source: Labour Dept *A Concise Guide to the Employment Ordinance* (Dec 2025) + Cap. 57 consolidated text (Jan 2026). **Not legal advice.**

## Scope

- **Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57)** applies to most HK employees (exceptions: family in same dwelling, certain overseas contracts, seafarers, registered apprentices).
- **All covered employees:** wage payment, deduction limits, statutory holidays.
- **Continuous contract employees:** rest days, paid annual leave, sickness allowance, severance, long service payment, etc.

## Continuous contract (from 18 Jan 2026)

Employee on continuous contract if:

1. Employed **4+ weeks** by same employer, **and**
2. Meets hours test: **≥17 hours/week** each week, **or** (if any week <17h) **≥68 hours** in that week + prior 3 weeks.

> Pre–18 Jan 2026: 18+ hours/week for 4 weeks.

**Burden of proof:** employer must prove contract is *not* continuous if disputed.

## Written contract essentials (Ch. 2)

Before employment, employer must inform employee clearly of:

1. **Wages** (rate, OT, allowances; piece/hour/day/week basis)
2. **Wage period**
3. **Notice** to terminate
4. **End-of-year payment** (if any) — amount/proportion and period

- Written contract → give employee a copy.
- Oral contract → provide written particulars if employee requests in writing.
- **Any term reducing EO rights is void.**

**Default:** continuous contract without express term = **month-to-month**, renewable.

**Records:** keep 12 months wage/employment history per employee (identity, job title, wages, hours if applicable, leave taken/paid, termination date, etc.).

## GM-relevant chapters (quick map)

| Topic | EO chapter | GM action |
|-------|------------|-----------|
| Wages / deductions | Ch. 3 | Pay on time; no unlawful deductions |
| Rest days, holidays, annual leave | Ch. 4 | Schedule + pay statutory entitlements |
| Sickness / maternity / paternity | Ch. 5–7 | Policy aligned to statutory minima |
| End of year payment | Ch.  8 | Define in contract if applicable |
| Termination / notice | Ch. 9 | Follow contract or EO defaults |
| Unfair dismissal protection | Ch. 10 | Document lawful reasons |
| Severance / long service | Ch. 11 | Budget on restructuring |
| Anti-union discrimination | Ch. 12 | HR policy check |
| MPF offset abolition | Appendix 2 | **No MPF offset against SP/LSP** (from 1 May 2025 transition rules in guide) |

## Termination (GM checklist)

1. Check **written contract notice** vs EO minimums.
2. Determine if **summary dismissal** grounds exist (serious misconduct — see EO Ch. 9).
3. Calculate **notice pay**, **annual leave balance**, **severance/LSP** if applicable.
4. Complete **written records** and exit documentation.
5. **Labour Tribunal / MECAB** exposure if unpaid awards (Ch. 13 employer criminal liability).

## PharmaEssentia HK subsidiary hooks

- Small HK office → every hire may trigger **continuous contract** quickly; part-time/agency rules differ.
- **Associate CPM / PM** roles: clarify hours, OT, and rest days in contract — avoid accidental continuous-contract surprises for contractors.
- Restructuring or role elimination → severance/LSP and **MPF** calculations need HR + counsel, not GM alone.
- GM **cannot sign all contracts** per role-gm — route employment contracts through authorized signatory + HR.

## When to escalate

- Disputed continuous contract status
- Summary dismissal of senior employee
- Collective redundancy / mass termination
- Any Labour Tribunal claim or union-related issue

**Official guide:** [labour.gov.hk — Concise Guide](https://www.labour.gov.hk/eng/public/ConciseGuide.htm)

## Shareholder vs stakeholder capitalism

| View | Advocate | Core claim |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Shareholder** | Friedman (1970) | Business social responsibility = increase profits within legal rules; CEOs aren't social policy experts |
| **Stakeholder** | Freeman | Firms create value for customers, employees, suppliers, communities, financiers — manage relationships for **sustainable** business |

**Managing for stakeholders (Freeman):**
- Value = financial **and** non-financial (fairness, values, world improvement)  
- Leaders must ensure core stakeholders receive value worth their continued support  
- History: Berle vs Dodd (1932) → managerial capitalism → 1970s–80s shareholder primacy resurgence  

**Debate nuance:**
- Friedman allows stakeholder actions when profit-maximizing — but warns "social responsibility" rhetoric invites regulation  
- Freeman minimizes "tradeoffs" language — agent should still **name tradeoffs explicitly** in GM decisions  
- **Persuasion asymmetry:** persuading others is hard; persuading ourselves is easy (debate experiment)

## Behavioral ethics (why ethics training alone fails)

## Conflict vs disagreement (Week 3)

### Not all disagreement → conflict

Conflict more likely when:
- **Action interdependence** — my choice affects your outcome  
- **Outcome importance** — high stakes  
- **Imbalanced evidence perception** — both sides think evidence favors them (**naïve realism**)  

### Naïve realism
- I see world objectively; reasonable people should agree with me  
- Disagreement attributed to others' ignorance, stupidity, or bad motives  
- **Fails to apply same bias to self**  

### Receptiveness to opposing views
Four components: (1) manage negative emotions (2) intellectual curiosity (3) respect toward opponents (4) tolerance of taboo topics  

**HEAR framework (appear receptive):**
- **H**edge claims ("I think it's possible…")  
- **E**mphasize agreement ("We both want…")  
- **A**cknowledge other perspectives ("I understand that…")  
- **R**eframe to positive ("I appreciate when…")  

**Caution:** appearing receptive ≠ being receptive (no correlation) — substance matters for HQ/clinical disputes.

**Field assignment finding:** people **overestimate** disagreement unpleasantness; post-discussion emotions better than expected — **learning** is possible if goal is understanding not persuasion.

## What strategy is (and is not)

**Strategy = a choice** about the company’s future — not fluff, not everything you do.

Must explain **HOW** you win via **sustainable competitive advantage** (not a fair fight).

### Six strategy questions (Strategy Triangle)

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **WHAT** will we sell? (products/services) |
| 2 | **To WHOM?** (customer segments) |
| 3 | **WHERE?** (markets/geographies) |
| 4 | **WHAT** do we offer that is **better than competitors**? (value proposition) |
| 5 | **HOW** will we deliver it **and make money**? (activity map / operating model) |
| 6 | **WHY** do we bother? (purpose / driving force) |

**Strategy Triangle:** align **product/market choices** (customer view) with **resources, core competences, values, processes** (internal view).

**Strategic thinking loop:** Understanding → Choice → Action → Measurement (continuous, not annual ritual).

**Hedgehog concept (Collins):** Strategy lives at the intersection of (1) deep passion, (2) what you can be **best in the world at**, and (3) what drives your **economic engine**. Use for personal career strategy and for testing whether a business unit has a coherent core.

**Strategy vs operational excellence:** OE = doing things **better** (efficiency). Strategy = doing **different** things or similar things **differently** — requires trade-offs (choosing what **not** to do).

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Objective** | Specific end goal + **time frame** (not mission/values fluff) |
| **Scope** | Boundaries — customers, products, geographies, channels you **will and won’t** serve |
| **Advantage** | **Value proposition** (why target customer buys you) + **activity alignment** (what only you can deliver) |

Requires **trade-offs** — you cannot win on everything.

## Customer & competitor analysis (Stage 1a–1c)

**Needs-based segmentation:** Segment by **jobs to be done** (functional + emotional), not demographics alone. Map buying journey and **full product** (core + expected + augmented).

**Basis of competition matrix:** Separate **hygiene factors** (table stakes — must meet minimum) from **basis of competition** (where better performance earns price premium). Don't confuse the two in positioning.

**Competitive asymmetries:** Map structural differences vs rivals — size, cost structure, channel, regulation, data — and design tactics that exploit asymmetries they cannot easily copy.

**Dangerous future competitors:** Look beyond direct rivals to adjacent players who could redefine the industry (e.g. tech platforms entering finance).

- **Does this fit?** (core vs peripheral; “we won’t do”)

## Portfolio & resource allocation

### BCG Matrix

| Quadrant | Typical stance |
|----------|----------------|
| **Stars** | Invest fully — growth + strength |
| **Cash cows** | Defend, harvest — fund stars |
| **Question marks** | Select 1–2 bets; avoid resource sinks |
| **Dogs** | Minimize; avoid sentimental reinvestment |

## Market entry & growth

### Core & adjacencies (growth model)

Expand via: new **products**, **customer segments**, **geographies**, **channels**, **value chain** positions — but only where advantage transfers.

### Entry logic

- Treat entry like **focused invasion** — concentration, momentum, **front pin in bowling alley**  
- Assess: Five Forces, profit pool, KSF, disruptive vs sustaining fit  
- **GE matrix** to prioritize adjacencies; gaps → build / partner / JV / acquire  

### Disruptive vs sustaining (Christensen)

| | Sustaining | Disruptive |
|---|------------|------------|
| Targets | Incumbent’s **best** customers | Incumbent’s **less profitable** customers |
| Model | Similar to incumbent | **Different** business model |
| Incumbent response | **Fight** with full resources | May **ignore** until too late |

**Attack options:** stealth · disruptive (flight not fight) · harmless (non-competing).

## Value disciplines (Treacy & Wiersema)

Win by being **best at one**, **good enough** on the other two:

| Discipline | Win through |
|------------|-------------|
| **Operational excellence** | Efficiency, reliability, best price-performance |
| **Customer intimacy** | Deep relationships, tailored solutions |
| **Product leadership** | Innovation, first-mover, superior product |

Misalignment (e.g. claiming product leadership while cutting R&D) breaks strategy.

## Scenarios & seeing the future

Scenarios are **thinking tools**, not predictions.

**Good scenario:** internally consistent, vivid, possible, implies **different actions**, widens view of futures, identifies **trigger metrics**.

**Test:** “Did I make better choices because of this?” — not “Did we predict correctly?”

**Method:** Pick **2 independent, high-impact uncertainties** → cross to **4 consistent scenarios** → for each ask: value chain shifts, capability gaps, white spaces, winners/losers, **no-regrets moves**, **leading indicators**.

**Strategy development process:** Understand today (customer, competitor, company, industry) → See future (trends, scenarios) → Generate alternatives → Evaluate & choose → Action plan.

## Digital future, trends & bets

**Trend matrix (signal vs noise):**

| Bucket | Stance |
|--------|--------|
| **No regrets** | Base case — do regardless of which future unfolds |
| **Big bets** | Invest when signal strengthens; high upside |
| **Long shots** | Contingency / insurance — small bets, option value |

**Observe extreme customers** today to spot mainstream tomorrow. Ask when disruptive tech becomes **“good enough”** and which **new business models** emerge.

**Reinforcing loops (flywheels):** Data loop (usage → data → personalization → value); **network effects** (more users → more value); **liquidity loop** on platforms (more supply ↔ more demand). These drive **winner-take-most** dynamics — plan for scale early where loops matter.

**Digital friction removal:** Distribution, transaction cost, and scale economics change — mid-tier undifferentiated players often **lose** profit; strongest brands and niches gain via demand-pull or global niche access.

**Digital business model patterns:** Freemium · asset-light · omnichannel · sharing economy · long tail · platforms (B2C/B2B/C2C) · everything-as-a-service · micropayments · mass customization.

---

## Platforms & multisided markets

Three defining features (Evans):

1. Serves **two or more interdependent customer groups**  
2. Value to each side **rises with size of the other side(s)**  
3. Platform provides superior **interaction** between groups  

**Chicken-and-egg:** Solve by subsidizing the harder side (e.g. free for women at dating club; charge merchants on credit cards).

**Pricing:** Direct costs often mislead — **load charges on the side that values participation less** or that you need to attract (marquee users/content).

**Platform playbook:** Start narrow (eBay + Pez), get pricing and kinks right, **then scale** — avoid premature 3G-style over-investment before content/liquidity exists.

**Common models:** **Razor/blade** (loss leader + high-margin consumables) · **Freemium** (free base → premium tier) · **Two-sided liquidity** (Windows, WeChat, marketplaces).

## International growth (Bain matrix)

**Reality check:** ~60% of international initiatives fail; ~33% marginal; ~17% solidly profitable.

**Two axes:** (1) **Economies of scale** — global vs local · (2) **Customer profile** — globally uniform vs locally distinct.

| Quadrant | Strategy |
|----------|----------|
| **Naturally local** | Don't need int'l; if abroad, one market at a time, local customization (supermarkets) |
| **Naturally global** | Think global Day 1; biggest markets; single brand/VP; central ops + local sales (aerospace) |
| **Customer-driven** | Follow global customers; common global VP + local service (ad agencies, B2B) |
| **Compelled by costs** | Modular product — global scale on components + local customization (automotive, entertainment) |

**Four conditions for profitable foreign expansion (Bain cheat sheet):** (1) strong **domestic core**, (2) **repeatable formula**, (3) **portable differentiation**, (4) **favorable host industry structure**.

**Vertical integration / value chain:** Session 5 asks where to play as boundaries shift (e.g. Netflix: content vs distribution vs devices).

---

## Evaluate alternatives (Stage 3)

For each option assess: **size of prize** (GE/attractiveness), **ability to win**, **partnerships** needed, **implementation barriers**. Use **fast/cheap experiments** for options not ready for full commitment.

**Core competencies (Prahalad & Hamel):** Underlying corporate capabilities (e.g. Honda engines) that are hard to copy, deliver customer value, and unlock **many markets** — build at corporate level, not only SBU silos.

**Poker vs roulette:** Roulette = small bets on everything. **Poker** = wait for **window of opportunity**, then **predatory leap** (big bet). Match bet sizing to conviction and industry clock speed.

**Generate alternatives like IDEO:** Split teams to explore **extreme options on one dimension each** — widens solution space before converging.

## Strategic planning (make it useful)

**Bad process:** annual template theater, junior dusts off last year, defend in review.

**Better process:** review when needed; CEO top-down guidance; debate **issues**; line + staff co-build; success = **new insight and alignment**.

**Critical link:** strategy must appear in **annual budget** and **leaders’ objectives** or it won’t happen.

---

## Implementing strategy (Stage 4)

**Describe (4a):** **BHAG** + explicit **trade-offs** — what you **stop** doing; **Attack / Defend / Exit** priorities per business.

**Plan action (4b):**

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|----------|
| **Capability gaps** | Close before scaling |
| **Catalytic mechanisms** | Simple enforceable rules that guarantee execution (Collins) |
| **Core values vs operating practices** | Values = timeless, never compromised; practices = adapt to environment — don't confuse when changing |
| **Initiatives & resources** | Balance horizons; **best people on biggest opportunities** — don't spread thin |
| **Alignment** | Budget reflects **new** allocation, not last year's sales targets; cascade to frontline |

**Measure & evolve (4c):** Track **leading indicators** (predictive), not only lagging. Off-track? Diagnose: **execution failure** vs **assumption failure** (experiments wrong) vs **strategic flaw** (VP/advantage obsolete).

**Imitation blockers (strategic fit depth):** 1st order consistency (Vanguard) → 2nd order reinforcing (Neutrogena) → 3rd order optimization (Gap) — **interlocked systems** resist copy.

**Strategic redesign triggers:** Don't wait for annual cycle — pivot when disruptive tech crosses "good enough," asymmetries shift, or scenarios' leading indicators fire.

**Business model vs implementation (Xiameter lesson):** **WHAT** decisions (strategy/model) separate from **HOW** decisions (org, channel conflict, cannibalization, change management).

## Entrepreneurial & innovation mindset

**Start-up ≠ small multinational:** Temporary org creating scalable model under **uncertainty** — needs different governance inside corporates.

**Analysis vs trials/learning:**

| Stable industry | Fast-changing industry |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| 3–5 yr plan, NPV gates | Monthly learning cycles |
| Incremental model change | Many fast cheap experiments |
| Targets realistic | Leading metrics; assumptions challengeable |

**Lean startup loop:** Hypotheses → experiments → measure → learn (Business Model Canvas). Prove **value hypothesis** and **growth hypothesis** before scaling — **premature scaling** is top killer.

**Focused innovation:** Innovate where it extends **value proposition / advantage activities** — not everywhere you merely match competitors.

**Christensen on corporates:** Start-ups should be **patient for growth, impatient for profit**; corporates often reverse this.

## 8 questions before a strategy recommendation

1. What is the **objective + timeframe**?  
2. What is **scope** (in/out)?  
3. What is our **advantage** — asset or capability, and is it durable?  
4. What does **Five Forces + value chain** say about industry profit?  
5. Does this fit **core** or distract from core?  
6. **Disruptive or sustaining** — how will incumbents respond?  
7. **Resource implication** — BCG/GE priority vs sticky budget?  
8. **Implementation** — budget, owners, metrics?  

---

## Do / don’t for this agent

**Do:** Name the choice and trade-off; tie recommendation to advantage and industry structure; separate strategy from operations.  
**Don’t:** Fluff words (world-class, synergies); recommend everything; ignore implementation/budget link; conflate mission with strategic objective.
