# MGMT6501Q — Communication for Business Leaders cheat sheet

> HKUST Spring 2026 · Pass/Fail · Distilled for **exec-comms playbook** · **Done 2026-07-27**  
> Attach when audience is HQ, board, C-suite, crisis comms, or difficult 1:1

---

## Core premise

**Business communication = sharing** thoughts so the audience **remembers, feels, and acts** as you intend.

**Success measures:**
- **Content:** Substantial + Consequential  
- **Delivery:** Efficient + Effective  

**Mindset:** Communication starts with **clear thinking and active listening**, not the mouth.

---

## Universal prep — three questions (always)

Before any message (email, speech, meeting, Q&A):

| # | Question | Label |
|---|----------|-------|
| 1 | What do I want them to **remember**? | Substantial |
| 2 | How do I want them to **feel**? | Effective |
| 3 | What do I want them to **do**? | Consequential |

After delivery: ask the audience the same three — **gap = improvement target**.

**Also confirm:** Audience · Channel (1:many / 1:1 / many:many) · Communication style · Success metric.

---

## Munter communication strategy (four lenses)

1. **Communicator strategy** — credibility, goals  
2. **Audience strategy** — knowledge, expectations, bias, resistance  
3. **Message strategy** — structure, tell/sell/consult/join  
4. **Channel choice** — medium fit  

**Styles:**

| Style | When |
|-------|------|
| **Tell** | Inform; low resistance |
| **Sell** | Persuade; need buy-in |
| **Consult** | Input before decision |
| **Join** | Co-create; high engagement |

## Feedback — ABCD model (giving)

- **Acceptable** · **Balanced** · **Constructive** · **Direct**  
- Substantial · Consequential  
- Describe **facts + impact** — not character attacks  
- Three conversions: Past→Future · Problem→Opportunity · Opinion→Suggestion  

**Receiving:** Feedback is a gift; clarify; don't defend; keep own judgment.

**Peer feedback triad:** (1) What do you remember? (2) How do you feel? (3) What will you do?

---

## One-to-one interviews — prep checklist

1. Objective?  
2. Style (tell/sell/consult/join)?  
3. Credibility with counterpart?  
4. What do they know/expect?  
5. Current situation & bias?  
6. Interest level in your message?  
7. Difficulty of requested action?  
8. What will persuade them?  
9. Message structure?  

**Observer role:** Manager's manager — escalation path visible.

**Appraisal pattern:** Hidden rating expectations → negotiate agreement → summarize next steps.

---

## Many-to-many — HBR presentation (2 min + Q&A)

**Summarization:** Distill article to **professional lessons** — not article replay.  
**Elaboration:** Deep-dive one concept (e.g., Chinese negotiation element) with examples.

**Roles:** Presenter · Q&A responder · Closer (90 sec)  
**Visual aids:** Support message — don't compete.

---

## Crisis comms — Tylenol simulation framework

### Internal crisis meeting

- Assign chair, timekeeper, minutes  
- Round 1: **What should we do?** (recall scope, tampering hypothesis, credo)  
- Round 2: **What do we say? Who speaks?**  
- Debrief meeting effectiveness  

### Press conference objectives (same three questions)

1. What should the world **remember**?  
2. How should people **feel**?  
3. What should they **do**?  

**Deliverables:** ≤90 sec prepared statement · named spokespeople · impromptu Q&A prep  

### Stakeholder tensions (generic crisis pattern)

| Role | Typical pull |
|------|--------------|
| CEO | Long-term trust, credo |
| Finance | Minimize liability, stock |
| Legal | Say little |
| PR/Comms | Fast public guidance |
| Medical | Public safety first |
| Marketing | Brand survival |
| Plant/Ops | Exonerate operations |

**J&J Credo anchor:** Patients/customers first — decision filter under conflict.

**Lesson:** Responsible leadership = **align words and actions** under multi-stakeholder stress; Q&A discipline matters as much as opening statement.

---

## Q&A and listening (Session 3 readings — concepts)

- **Five kinds of listening** (HBR): discriminate content, understand, remember, evaluate, respond — match mode to question type  
- **Promise-based management:** Execution via explicit commitments between parties  
- **Communication that blocks learning:** Defensive routines, mixed messages — diagnose before persuading  

---

```markdown
## Ask (one sentence)
## Why now
## Recommendation
## Options considered (2–3 lines)
## Risks / asks of HQ
## Decision needed by (date)
```

# EWMBA 296.2F — Virtual Leadership cheat sheet

> Haas / GNAM Fall 2026 · Caleshu & Charnsupharindr · Distilled 2026-10-17  
> Work use: virtual presence, inclusive meetings, cross-cultural teams.  
> Class use: read [study-mode.md](study-mode.md) first. Do not paste this file into Rumi assignments.

---

## Core premise (from the decks)

Authentic leadership is five powers at once — **Intention, Presence, Communication, Knowledge, Conviction** (Mark Rittenberg).  
Communication is **Words + Voice + Non-verbal**, judged by whether **intention matches impact**.  
Tech setup supports presence. It does not replace it.

**Trust** (Maister, Green & Galford; slide spells the third name “Gelford”):

```
Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation
```

Raise the numerator with proof, follow-through, and a real human moment. The fastest way to destroy trust on a call is to make the hour about you.

---

## Six outcomes ? what to do on camera

| Outcome | Do this |
|---------|---------|
| **1. Virtual presence** | Eye line at the lens. One idea per breath. Gesture inside the frame. Name your intention before you speak. |
| **2. Inclusive meetings** | Airtime is a design choice. Seed, then invite. Written prompt plus spoken prompt. Someone watches chat. |
| **3. Persuasive presentations** | Vertical takeoff ? middle ? end ? one clear takeaway. Vocal variety. Deep and brief. |
| **4. Cross-cultural / time zones** | Plot the gap on Meyer’s map. Decide sync vs async before the invite. Livermore: Western defaults can backfire. |
| **5. Read the room** | Watch faces, chat, silence, and who has not spoken. Change the move, not the volume. |
| **6. Team agreements** | Roles, cadence, time-zone rule, and how dissent is invited. Aristotle: how the team works beats who is on it. |

```
VERTICAL TAKEOFF  ?  Beginning  ?  Middle  ?  End  ?  CLEAR TAKEAWAY
                         vocal variety throughout
                         deep & brief (class capture — confirm wording)
```

- **Takeoff:** a question, a fact, or a scene. No agenda recital.
- **One takeaway** the room can repeat after you leave.
- Homework named on the Week 1 deck: heirloom story; watch Amanda Gorman and Chimamanda Adichie; read Burkus and Zaki.

## 20-minute inclusive meeting (Week 4)

| Part | Job | Time shape |
|------|-----|------------|
| **1** | Vertical takeoff ? the why | Open |
| **2** | Warm-up | Short, everyone can answer |
| **3** | Interactive exercise | The work of the meeting |
| **4** | Debrief ? honorable closure | One commitment, not a summary speech |

**Before you design it (Priya Parker, on the deck):**

1. Purpose — most important need of this audience?  
2. Context — how do we build a shared room?  
3. Ritual — how do we connect fast, and how do we close on purpose?  
4. Informal moment — what is unscripted?  
5. Rules and roles — who does what, including the chat watcher?

Haas principles assigned across eight groups (two groups each): **Question the Status Quo · Confidence Without Attitude · Students Always · Beyond Yourself.**

A “magical question” (Parker, Week 4): everyone wants to answer it, and everyone wants to hear the answers. What works in one room can fail in another. Prompts are on slides 11–12; pick one, do not read the list aloud.

## Team effectiveness (Week 4)

**Google Project Aristotle — did not predict effectiveness:** sitting together, consensus, extroversion, individual performance, workload, seniority, team size, tenure.

**Did matter:** psychological safety ? dependability ? structure & clarity ? meaning ? impact.

**Edmondson:** safety is “the soil, not the seed.”

|  | Low accountability | High accountability |
|--|--------------------|---------------------|
| **High safety** | Comfort | **Learning** |
| **Low safety** | Apathy | Anxiety |

**Lencioni stack (bottom up):** absence of trust ? fear of conflict ? lack of commitment ? avoidance of accountability ? inattention to results.

**Arrien, four-fold way:** show up · pay attention to what has heart · tell the truth without blame · stay open to outcome, not attached to it.

**Competence curve:** unconscious incompetent ? conscious incompetent ? conscious competent ? unconscious competent. Name the stage when feedback stings; that is the work.

---

## Super-facilitator (Zaki) — three moves

Collective intelligence (Woolley) comes from **how** the group interacts, not from stacking stars.

1. **Attunement** — read who is in, who is out, who is hot.  
2. **Communication** — say you expect them to build on each other.  
3. **Distribution** — do not eat the airtime. Pass so others score.

Playbook: role-craft to strengths · state your belief in them · keep the ball moving · catalytic questions (“what role is this playing?”) instead of verdicts. Directive wins the first hour. Empowering wins the hard problem.

## Hybrid energy (Gardner) — diagnose before you pep-talk

| Path | The team is… | Leader move |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| **Channel** | Busy, scattered, over-meetinged | One overarching goal. Do not monitor inputs. Check fundamental attribution error before calling someone “checked out.” |
| **Generate** | Actually depleted | Co-create meaning. Protect dignity, not just output. Small progress (Amabile). |
| **Multiply** | Capable but flat | Show a real idiosyncrasy. Notice sides of people you do not usually use. |

Energizing leaders in her research: compassion, humility, generous recognition — not a personality type.

---

## Loneliness (Montañez)

Three distances: **physical · operational · affinity.** You can always work the third.

1. Risks / respect / each person’s relationship to remote work  
2. Recognition that names the act, what you experienced, and the impact  
3. Career and mentoring paths that are not “be in the room”  
4. Model a whole person: replace empty “how are you?”, leave room for friendship, make mental health speakable

---

## Culture — do not lead on one stereotype (Meyer + Livermore)

**Meyer’s eight scales** (position is relative, not a national verdict):

| Scale | Poles |
|-------|--------|
| Communicating | Low-context ? high-context |
| Evaluating | Direct negative feedback ? diplomatic |
| Persuading | Principles-first ? applications-first (also specific ? holistic) |
| Leading | Egalitarian ? hierarchical |
| Deciding | Consensual ? top-down |
| Trusting | Task-based ? relationship-based |
| Disagreeing | Confrontational ? avoidance |
| Scheduling | Linear ? flexible |

Communicating and Evaluating are different scales. A culture can be implicit and still blunt.

**Livermore — four Western defaults that derail global teams:** too much autonomy · too much psychological safety (copied without the local meaning) · too much emphasis on differences · too much transparency. Cultural intelligence is a larger toolbox, not a one-time briefing.

**Sync vs async:** decide from the scale that is actually in conflict (scheduling, deciding, trusting), not from habit.

---

## In the moment (Wadhwa + Galinsky + Hewlett)

**Five energies** (Wadhwa): Purpose · Wisdom · Growth · Love · Self-realization. Pick one or two actions for a specific call. Do not perform a style you do not feel — Thatcher would have noticed.

**Inspiring leaders** (Galinsky): visionary, exemplar, mentor. Vision is values-based and image-rich, and it is repeated. Exemplar is calm under heat. Mentor shares the lead. Practice loop: reflect ? emulate ? intend ? do it in ordinary meetings.

**Executive presence now** (Hewlett): gravitas, communication, appearance. Confidence and decisiveness still anchor gravitas. What rose: inclusiveness, respect, listen-to-learn, command of Zoom, authenticity, and a presence that works online and in person. People still need to feel seen, heard, and valued.

---

## Creativity traps (Burkus) — for brainstorms on Zoom

1. Brainstorming is only the divergent step. Research the question first. Converge, prototype, and test after.  
2. Too much cohesion. Task conflict is a signal. Referee it; do not shut it down.  
3. Judging before evidence. Test, then pitch.

## Five pillars of responsible leadership (course definition)

| # | Pillar | Behavior |
|---|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Inclusive, long-term decision-making** | Consult early; accept slower timelines to protect quality/trust |
| 2 | **Principles & integrity** | Protect trust even when costly or unpopular |
| 3 | **Accountability & ownership** | Own failures; no scapegoating |
| 4 | **Empathy & stakeholder inclusivity** | Open dialogue; multiple beneficiary groups |
| 5 | **Servant leadership & empowerment** | Delegate with trust; develop capability |

**When things go wrong:** poor communication, blame-shifting, micromanagement, singular financial focus → economic + career + human costs.

## Shareholder vs stakeholder capitalism

| View | Advocate | Core claim |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Shareholder** | Friedman (1970) | Business social responsibility = increase profits within legal rules; CEOs aren't social policy experts |
| **Stakeholder** | Freeman | Firms create value for customers, employees, suppliers, communities, financiers — manage relationships for **sustainable** business |

**Managing for stakeholders (Freeman):**
- Value = financial **and** non-financial (fairness, values, world improvement)  
- Leaders must ensure core stakeholders receive value worth their continued support  
- History: Berle vs Dodd (1932) → managerial capitalism → 1970s–80s shareholder primacy resurgence  

**Debate nuance:**
- Friedman allows stakeholder actions when profit-maximizing — but warns "social responsibility" rhetoric invites regulation  
- Freeman minimizes "tradeoffs" language — agent should still **name tradeoffs explicitly** in GM decisions  
- **Persuasion asymmetry:** persuading others is hard; persuading ourselves is easy (debate experiment)  

---

## Behavioral ethics (why ethics training alone fails)

| Concept | Mechanism | GM/PM example |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Prediction error** | We underestimate situational pressure | Approving borderline claims under launch pressure |
| **Ethical fading** | Problem-solving frame hides ethical dimension | "Maximize engagement" → inappropriate HCP targeting |
| **Bounded ethicality** | Good people cheat a little, not maximally | Slight efficacy implication stretch in internal decks |
| **Self-concept maintenance** | Small violations to preserve "good person" identity | Low-grade data cherry-picking |
| **Crowding out** | Small incentives/fines replace intrinsic motivation | KPI bonuses distort patient-first behavior |

**Traditional vs behavioral:**

| Traditional | Behavioral |
|-------------|------------|
| Teach utilitarianism, deontology, virtue | Examine psychology of real decisions |
| Assume knowledge gap | Assume motivation + context gap |
| Honor codes, ESG hours | Design systems, nudges, accountability |

**Trolley vs bridge:** utilitarian (aggregate good) vs deontological (don't use person as means) — most people flip switch but resist push; **doctrine of double effect**.

---

## ESG — bright side and dark side

### Bright side (BlackRock / purpose narrative)
- Link purpose to profit; stakeholder alignment can motivate employees and reduce risk  

### Dark side (Hagmann research + Tariq Fancy critique)
| Risk | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| **Convenient facts** | Stakeholder capitalism feels easier than regulation/taxes |
| **Nudge substitution** | Learning about soft nudges **reduces** support for tough policy (climate, retirement) |
| **Attention limits** | Individual carbon footprint campaigns (BP origin) shift blame from firms/government |
| **Strategic misdirection** | Firms fund literacy/obesity research while benefiting from core harm |
| **ESG theater** | Net-zero pledges without structural change |

**Agent rule:** when HQ asks for "ESG narrative," separate **material impact** from **reputational positioning** — cite specific metrics and owners.

**Turing / Valeant lesson:** in fragmented buyer markets with weak reputational penalty, **pricing ethics fail structurally** — leadership can't rely on shame alone; regulation/market structure matter.

**Merck Mectizan counter-example:** genuine long-term stakeholder investment possible — but same firm can also behave oppositely elsewhere.

## Conflict vs disagreement (Week 3)

### Not all disagreement → conflict

Conflict more likely when:
- **Action interdependence** — my choice affects your outcome  
- **Outcome importance** — high stakes  
- **Imbalanced evidence perception** — both sides think evidence favors them (**naïve realism**)  

### Naïve realism
- I see world objectively; reasonable people should agree with me  
- Disagreement attributed to others' ignorance, stupidity, or bad motives  
- **Fails to apply same bias to self**  

### Receptiveness to opposing views
Four components: (1) manage negative emotions (2) intellectual curiosity (3) respect toward opponents (4) tolerance of taboo topics  

**HEAR framework (appear receptive):**
- **H**edge claims ("I think it's possible…")  
- **E**mphasize agreement ("We both want…")  
- **A**cknowledge other perspectives ("I understand that…")  
- **R**eframe to positive ("I appreciate when…")  

**Caution:** appearing receptive ≠ being receptive (no correlation) — substance matters for HQ/clinical disputes.

**Field assignment finding:** people **overestimate** disagreement unpleasantness; post-discussion emotions better than expected — **learning** is possible if goal is understanding not persuasion.

## Organizational culture (Week 4)

- Culture = work **environment** (physical, peers, support, family spillover)  
- Bad environments often unnoticed until compared to good ones  
- **Responsible leader as architect/therapist** — design environment, not only exhort values  
- NovaBridge simulation: values statement + cross-functional hidden information → majority vote outcomes affect all  
- Everyone influences culture — not only CEO  

**Rob Parson / Morgan Stanley (case theme):** star performer vs culture fit — revenue vs collaboration norms.
