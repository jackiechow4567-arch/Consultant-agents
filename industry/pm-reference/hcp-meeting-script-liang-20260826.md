## Page-by-page script (exact lines)

### CHR — “significant and sustained hematologic control”

**CHR on this page:** no phlebotomy in 3 months; Hct &lt;45%; WBC &lt;10; Plt &lt;400.

**Point at the graph, do not skip month 12:**

“On **this curve**, hydroxyurea is ahead at 12 months — about **75% vs 62%**. That is the pharmacology: HU is faster. Mean titration in the PI is about **2.6 vs 3.7 months**. If you need counts down this month to stop thrombosis or bleeding, HU may still be the first tool.

The lines **cross by 24 months**. At 60 months the ropeg curve is about **73%** vs about **53%** on HU/BAT. That is the long-term CHR story **on this page**.”

**Point at 84.3% (107/127):**

“This **84.3%** is **not** that graph. The footnote is ‘criteria met **at any time**’ by 36 months — **ever-response**, 107 of 127. I will not tell you 84% are still in CHR at three years.”

**Then the PI, because he will know PROUD-PV:**

“The **registered 12-month primary** in the Hong Kong PI is **43.1% vs 46.0%, p=0.63**. Non-inferiority was **not met**. That analysis counts discontinuations as failures. Both numbers can be true: the graph is the leave-behind curve; 43% is the ITT primary.

The PI-aligned late primary from Gisslinger 2023 is **54.5% vs 34.9% at 72 months, p=0.02**. LOCF 72.6% vs 47.3% is the published analogue of this ~70% visual.”

**4/127 box:**

“Major thromboembolic events **3% (4/127)** at 36 months on this page. That is **not** the six-year EFS count of 5/95 — different set, different time, different composite.”

### JAK2 — “allele burden”

“Median VAF **37% down to 8%** on ropeg at 60 months. HU/BAT **38% to 44%** — it rebounds. Higher mutant burden is associated with thrombosis, hemorrhage, and fibrotic progression. I am **not** saying you can stop the drug at 8%, and I am not claiming treatment-free remission.”

If he says “selectively depletes HSCs”: stay on the page; do not expand to “eradicates the clone.”

### EFS KM

“Event-free survival over at least six years: first risk events **5 in 95 vs 12 in 74, p=0.04**. Events on this page are **progression, death, and thromboembolic events**. This is **not** overall survival. I will not say it prevents leukemia — the AML cases in this analysis sat in the control arm and the numbers are small.”

If he wants the KM number: probability **0.94 vs 0.82**, HR **0.34**.

### Safety page 9

“This table is **all-causality**, 36 months, 127 vs 127.

Cytopenias are **not** worse than control — thrombocytopenia 21% vs 28%, leukopenia 18% vs 22%, anaemia 13% vs 24%. Fatigue 13% both.

The IFN signature is **liver enzymes**: GGT **16% vs 2%**, grade ≥3 **8% vs 2%**; ALT 13% vs 2%; AST 10% vs 2%; arthralgia 12% vs 4%.

Separately, **drug-related discontinuation** is about **11% vs 2–3%**. That is why we titrate by the PI and monitor LFTs and mood — not because grade ≥3 treatment-related events were higher (~16% both arms).”

Do **not** quote Japan Ph2 alopecia 55.2% unless he asks about Asian hair loss; PI lists alopecia around 6–7%.

### PI page

“Start **100 micrograms every two weeks**, or **50** if already on another cytoreductive, or if GFR is 15–29. Up by 50 every two weeks to Hct &lt;45%, platelets &lt;400, WBC &lt;10. Max 500 every two weeks. After at least 1.5 years stable, the interval **may** go to every four weeks.

**Not** the 250–350–500 HIDAT schedule — that is not the Hong Kong label.

Contraindications on this page include **thyroid disease unless controlled**, decompensated cirrhosis, ESRD, severe psychiatric disease, severe uncontrolled CV disease, autoimmune disease, transplant immunosuppression, telbivudine.”
