## Recommendation (training design)

**Do train two visit types — (A) private IFN-experienced clinic and (B) HA consultant — with nurse, wastage, PSP math, and PV first-patient scripts. Do not keep a HU-first academic slide curriculum as the core.**

Why (from Mar–Aug 2026 field contacts, de-identified in `field-visit-patterns.md`):

1. Almost every HCP listed affordability as a **Barrier**. They already use **Pegasys or China IFN**. They do not ask for CONTINUATION-PV p-values first.
2. The close that worked was **multi-visit + nurse + first patient**, not a single scientific dump. The close that failed was **q4w-to-save-cost**, **HQ will cut price**, **250 mcg as a discount**, and **ET/MF is easier to start**.
3. Pegasys is **back in Hong Kong**. Shortage is no longer the whole story. Residual honest POD is **PV label + supply reliability you can document + PSP rules + 6-year vs-HU data** — not potency or flu-like vs Pegasys.

**Explicit non-goals**

- Not a substitute for Medical Information letters  
- Not permission to originate local scientific claims  
- Not ET/MF promotional enablement  
- Not a congress-sponsorship sales pitch  
- Not a promise that HQ will lower list price

# Track A — Private IFN clinic (core)

**Success:** Physician names **one on-label PV patient**; nurse can inject; first-patient follow-up date exists; you did not invent a SKU, pen, or price cut.

## 45-second opening (Pegasys-experienced private haematologist)

> “You already use interferon in PV. BESREMi is **ropeginterferon alfa-2b**, indicated in Hong Kong as **monotherapy for adults with polycythaemia vera without symptomatic splenomegaly** — that is the labelled difference versus off-label peginterferon alfa-2a.  
> Dosing is **every two weeks**, start **100 micrograms** (50 if already on another cytoreductive), +50 to count targets, **maximum 500**. Many patients in the Phase 3 program sat near **350–380**, not 500.  
> I will not claim it is more potent or better tolerated than Pegasys — we have **no PV head-to-head**, and the PI forbids potency comparisons.  
> Access is private plus **written PSP rules**; I will not quote a special price. If supply reliability of other interferons is the clinical problem, I can speak only to **our** current availability, not to their catalogue.  
> What would help is: which **PV** patient you would even consider, and whether **wastage at low titration doses** is the blocker I should take to Medical/RA.”

## PSP one-liner (memorize)

> “List is on the order of **HK$36,000 per injection**. Private Sector is **buy 2 get 1**, private only, first year, **expires end of 2026**. Base Case is **buy 5 get 1**. I will confirm eligibility with market access before I apply it to a named patient. Clinic overhead is **their** fee — I will not invent a net.”

## Q17. “Pegasys is about a thousand dollars a syringe. Yours is thirty times.”

**Say:**  
“The gap is real. I will not pretend PSP closes it to peg-IFN. What I can put next to that gap is: **on-label PV**, **q2w labelled schedule**, **six-year vs-HU data**, and **PSP as written**. ## Q20. “Pegasys is back. Why switch? Their supply was the only problem.”

**Say:**  
“If supply has resumed, shortage is not a reason to switch today. Reasons that remain inside the label: **PV indication in HK**, **dosing schedule**, **named long-term vs-HU evidence**. If a **specific** presentation of another IFN is still stocked out, that is an operational fact for **that product**, not proof we are clinically superior.”

## Q21. “What dose does [famous doctor] use? 250 then 350 then 500?”

**Say:**  
“I will not turn another clinician’s practice into our protocol. The HK PI starts at **100 mcg** (50 if on other cytoreductive), +50 mcg every two weeks, max **500**. Any **accelerated** start at 250 is a **Medical** question, not a sales titration.”

# Module 0 — Compliance spine (do this first)

**Success:** You can stop a conversation in one sentence without sounding evasive.

## Hard constraints (always win)

| Constraint | Operational meaning in a clinic |
|------------|----------------------------------|
| HK PI indication | **Monotherapy in adults** for **PV without symptomatic splenomegaly** |
| On-label only (proactive) | You may raise PV. You may **not** raise ET, MF, or other indications |
| ET/MF | **Reactive scientific exchange only**; log in Veeva; cite current local PI; escalate to Medical |
| Comparative claims (HKAPI §3.5) | Named comparator + adequate, well-controlled, statistically significant study |
| IFN-to-IFN | **No superiority.** PI: potency of this product **must not be compared** to another pegylated or non-pegylated protein of the same class |
| Superlatives | No unqualified “safe,” “best,” “only,” “guaranteed” |
| Hospitality | Breakfast/lunch **HK$400**/attendee; dinner **HK$800**; no entertainment; no POM-branded giveaways |
| Promo pieces | Abbreviated PI (indication, dosage, contraindications, side effects, **approval date**) |
| Advisory / symposium | ≥ **two-thirds** genuine scientific content; agenda in advance |
| AI drafts | Still need full lexicon + compliance review before any HCP use |

## STOP–BRIDGE–CITE (use on every hard question)

1. **STOP** — Classify the ask: *on-label efficacy / safety / access / off-label / comparative IFN / listing date*.
2. **BRIDGE** — Name the clinical concern in their language (“You’re asking how this sits versus HU for long-term events…”).
3. **CITE** — Answer only from PI or a **named** study. If you cannot cite, say so and offer Medical follow-up.

**One-liners that keep trust:**

- “I can speak to the Hong Kong indication and the named studies in our PI. For anything beyond that, I’ll bring Medical.”
- “We do not have a head-to-head versus [Pegasys / China ropeg / traditional IFN], and our PI says potency should not be compared across that class.”
- “I won’t over-promise on Hospital Authority listing. I can walk through the evidence we *are* packaging.”

## Ethical fading watch (MGMT5590)

The failure mode is not a villainous off-label pitch. It is **“just this once”** under a senior hematologist’s pressure: a slight IFN comparison, a hint on ET, a soft listing date. Treat that as a **system** problem: if you cannot cite it in this playbook, you do not say it.

# Module 1 — Product identity (what BESREMi *is*)

## Product facts (HK PI)

| Item | Say this |
|------|----------|
| Name / INN | Besremi®, **ropeginterferon alfa-2b** |
| Form | Solution for injection, prefilled syringe **500 mcg/mL** (1 mL); SC; adjustable **50–500 mcg** |
| Indication | **Monotherapy, adults, polycythaemia vera without symptomatic splenomegaly** |
| Excipients of note | Benzyl alcohol 10 mg/mL; polysorbate 80 0.05 mg/mL |
| ADR channel | Drug Office ADR Online: http://www.drugoffice.gov.hk/adr.html |

## Dosing (summary — always defer to PI for the individual patient)

| Phase | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Start | **100 mcg** SC; **50 mcg** if already on other cytoreductive therapy |
| Titrate | **+50 mcg every 2 weeks**; taper other cytoreductive in parallel |
| Targets | Hct **<45%**, platelets **<400×10⁹/L**, WBC **<10×10⁹/L** |
| Max | **500 mcg every 2 weeks** |
| Maintenance | Every 2 weeks for **≥1.5 years** at a stable dose; **may extend to every 4 weeks** |

**Clinical nuance you must own (not hide):** PI notes mean titration ~**3.7 months** for ropeginterferon vs ~**2.6 months** for hydroxycarbamide. HU may be preferred when **early** count reduction is needed to prevent thrombosis/bleeding. This is a **credibility** point, not an anti-product point.

## Contraindications (memorize the clusters)

Decompensated cirrhosis (Child-Pugh B/C); ESRD (GFR <15); severe psychiatric history; severe uncontrolled cardiovascular disease; autoimmune disease; transplant immunosuppression; combination with **telbivudine**; hypersensitivity.

**Physician translation:** interferon-class risks are real. Do not say “well tolerated” as a blanket claim. Offer the PI ADR section and PV process.

## Who is *not* the on-label patient (proactive)

- Symptomatic splenomegaly
- Pediatric
- ET, prefibrotic/overt MF, or “MPN unspecified”
- Combination regimens not in the HK PI (do not invent)

# Module 2 — Evidence you can say (and the wall)

## Claim wall

| Comparator | Head-to-head in HK conversation? | Source |
|------------|----------------------------------|--------|
| Hydroxyurea / hydroxycarbamide | **Yes, if you name the RCT and the stats** | PROUD-PV / CONTINUATION-PV (also in PI) |
| Phlebotomy alone (low-risk PV) | **Yes, if you name the RCT and the stats** | Low-PV, NEJM Evidence 2023 |
| Pegasys / other IFN / China ropeginterferon | **No PV superiority** | **No PV H2H.** Hepatitis H2H vs Pegasys exists but is **off-indication** — see `pegasys-claim-check.md`. PI potency warning still bars mcg-to-mcg comparisons |
| Anagrelide | **No** | No cited RCT in the PM library |
| Ruxolitinib | **No uncited superiority** | Different role (typically HU-resistant/intolerant in broader MPN practice) — do not invent a BESREMi-vs-rux RCT |

## PROUD-PV / CONTINUATION-PV (Gisslinger et al., *Leukemia* 2023)

Use for: “Why not stay on HU?” and disease-modification narrative.

| Point | Number (cite the paper) |
|-------|-------------------------|
| Design | Phase III, ropeginterferon alfa-2b vs HU (control → BAT allowed in extension); ~6 years |
| Disease response at 12 months (PI) | **43.1%** (ropeg arm); n=254, 1:1 |
| CHR at 72 months | **54.5% vs 34.9%** (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.07–2.26, **p=0.02**) |
| Event-free survival at 72 months | **0.94 vs 0.82** (log-rank **p=0.04**); HR **0.34** (95% CI 0.12–0.97) |
| Risk events | 5/95 (**5.3%**) vs 12/74 (**16.2%**) — mix of thrombosis, MF, AML, death; do not collapse into “prevents cancer” |
| JAK2V617F molecular response (ELN) at 6 years | **66.0% vs 19.4%** (**p<0.0001**) |

**Say:** “In the named Phase III program versus hydroxyurea, complete hematologic response and event-free survival at six years favored ropeginterferon, with a documented molecular response difference.”  
**Do not say:** “It is safer than HU” / “It prevents MF and AML” as unqualified claims.

## Low-PV (Barbui et al., *NEJM Evidence* 2023)

Use for: younger / low-risk / “phlebotomy is enough.”

| Point | Number |
|-------|--------|
| Design | Phase 2, open-label; ropeg **100 mcg q2w** vs phlebotomy-only; n=127 |
| Primary (Hct ≤45% over 12 months) | **81% vs 51%** |
| Phlebotomy burden | Lower in responders; non-responders who switched needed high phlebotomy (library cites **4.7**/patient/year in abstract context — quote only if you have the paper in hand) |
| Symptoms / spleen | Lower moderate/severe symptoms and splenomegaly among ropeg responders |

**Say:** “In low-risk PV, the named Low-PV study showed better hematocrit control versus phlebotomy alone.”  
**Do not say:** “All low-risk patients should start BESREMi” as a guideline you invented.

## PARADIGM / Taiwan HIDAT / other RWE

- **PARADIGM (Phase 4):** referenced in role memory as a lexicon source — **do not expand locally** beyond cleared materials.
- **Regional RWE / Taiwan epidemiology:** market-sizing or Medical-owned; not a proactive efficacy claim.
- **Pregnancy case reports:** typically **not** promotional. Refer to PI + Medical.

### Q1. “Why is this so expensive versus traditional interferon / Pegasys / China ropeg?”

**They mean:** Justify premium without a head-to-head.

**Say:**  
“Price is a fair question. We do **not** have a head-to-head versus those interferons, and the Hong Kong PI states that potency should not be compared across pegylated or non-pegylated proteins in this class. What I *can* show is the **PV-specific indication**, the **dosing schedule**, and the **named studies versus hydroxyurea and versus phlebotomy alone**. For an individual patient’s affordability, we have structured patient-support options I can outline with the eligibility rules — I will not invent a discount.”

**Never say:** “More effective than Pegasys.” “Pegasys is 3× lower dose.” “We have less flu-like than Pegasys.” “Better quality than China ropeg.” “Worth 10× because…”

**If they press:** Use the difference script in Q1b, then Medical Information. Do not fill silence with a hepatitis comparison.

**After:** Log competitive question type (not patient identifiers) for brand/Medical. Do not put unpublished pricing strategy in the note.

### Q1b. “What is the difference from Pegasys?” (dose / flu-like / old hep studies)

**They mean:** Translate 500 mcg vs 180 mcg into “yours is stronger / better tolerated.”

**Checked facts (do not over-claim):** Full table in `industry/pm-reference/pegasys-claim-check.md`.

| Piece | True? | Clinic use |
|-------|-------|------------|
| BESREMi max **500 mcg q2w** | Yes | Yes, as **maximum** |
| Pegasys adult hep label **180 mcg weekly** | Yes | As *their* labelled hep schedule only |
| “3× lower dose” (180 vs 500) | **Misleading** — ignores weekly vs q2w; 2-week mass is ~360 vs 500 mcg; same-mcg PK is not equivalent | **No** |
| “500 mcg = optimal” | No — study **means ~350–380 mcg**; 500 is the cap | Say max, not optimal |
| Hep C Phase 2 vs **Pegasys** (flu-like numerically lower) | Study exists; HCV + ribavirin; small n; **not PV** | Medical-reactive only |
| Hep B Phase 1/2 vs **Pegasys** | Study exists; authors said **comparable** safety; rash, not flu-like, was the standout AE | Medical-reactive only |
| Hep C Phase 3 | Vs **PEG-Intron**, not Pegasys | Do not mis-cite |
| Less flu-like than Pegasys **in PV** | **No PV H2H**; BESREMi PV label still lists flu-like as common | **No** |
| Used up to **6 years** in PROUD/CONTI | Yes — 72-month follow-up vs HU | **Yes** |

**Say:**  
“Different molecules, different labels. BESREMi is the PV-indicated product here: every two weeks, titrate to counts, **maximum 500 micrograms** — in the Phase 3 program the **mean** dose was about **350–380 micrograms**, not 500 for every patient. Pegasys is alfa-2a; its classic adult hepatitis dose is **180 micrograms once weekly**. Those microgram numbers are **not** a 3× potency comparison — our PI says not to compare potency in this class, and the dosing intervals differ. We do not have a PV head-to-head versus Pegasys. Historical hepatitis studies are a Medical Information topic, not a PV promotional comparison. What I can show in PV is PROUD-PV / CONTINUATION-PV versus hydroxyurea, with patients followed **through six years**. Flu-like symptoms remain an interferon-class effect on our label.”

**Never say:** “Pegasys is three times weaker.” “We already won on flu-like in Hep B/C.” “Phase 3 hep was versus Pegasys.”

### Q2. “I already use HU. Why switch?”

**They mean:** Incremental benefit vs familiarity and cost.

**Say:**  
“HU remains appropriate when you need **faster** count control — the PI notes a shorter mean titration phase with hydroxycarbamide (~2.6 vs ~3.7 months). Where the conversation is **durable hematologic control, event-free survival, and molecular response over years**, CONTINUATION-PV reported CHR 54.5% vs 34.9% at 72 months (p=0.02) and EFS 0.94 vs 0.82 (p=0.04). Switching is a clinical judgment, not a slogan.”

**Never say:** “HU is outdated.” “Everyone should switch.”

### Q11. “When will this be on the HA formulary?”

**Say:**  
“Public listing is our primary access goal. I will not give you a date I do not have. The dossier is PV-only evidence plus budget impact in a cost-contained HA environment. If you have clinical questions that would strengthen appropriate-use language, I will take them back to Medical/Market Access — not as a paid ‘thank you,’ but as scientific input through proper channels.”

**Never say:** “Next cycle, definitely.” “Once we have your support it will list.”

---

### Q12. “Can we go to every four weeks?”

**Say:**  
“PI: after at least **1.5 years** at a **stable** dose on every-two-week dosing, the interval **may** extend to every four weeks. That is a labelled option, not a promise that every patient will get there.”

---

### Q13. “Pregnancy / family planning?”

**Say:**  
“That is a Medical/PI question, not a promotional one. I will not use case reports as a selling point. I will get you the labelled language and a Medical follow-up.”

---

### Q14. “How does this compare with ruxolitinib / anagrelide?”

**Say:**  
“Different mechanisms and typically different places in PV care. I will not make an uncited superiority claim versus ruxolitinib or anagrelide. If you want a Medical comparison of labelled indications and published trials, I will request it.”

---

### Q15. “I heard supply issues with other IFNs. Should I switch everyone now?”

**Say:**  
“Availability of alternatives can matter operationally. It is **not** a clinical superiority argument. If we have current BESREMi supply status from the company, I can share the factual availability statement we are allowed to use — I will not weaponize a competitor shortage.”

---

### Q16. “Can I use it with HU or other drugs?”

**Say:**  
“The HK indication is **monotherapy**. Titration includes **tapering** other cytoreductive therapy. I will not recommend an off-label combination protocol.”

Dosing starts at 100 micrograms — 50 if they are already on another cytoreductive — titrated by 50 every two weeks toward standard count targets, up to 500 every two weeks; some stable patients may later move to every four weeks after a year and a half.

# Module 4 — Access story without overpromising

**Patient-journey narrative (internal):** private diagnosis → PSP-supported treatment → hoped-for HA Drug List. Use with HCPs only as **process**, not as a promise.

| Channel | What you may discuss | What you may not |
|---------|----------------------|------------------|
| Private hematology / HKSH and private clinics | Label, evidence, **Private Sector PSP** (buy 2 get 1, first year, **ends 2026**) | Exception pricing; entertainment-heavy events |
| Mix / other private | **Base Case** PSP (buy 5 get 1) | Blurring the two programs |
| HA cluster hospitals (QMH, QEH, PWH, PYEH, UCH, TMH, TKOH, etc.) | Evidence quality, budget-impact *logic*, current **non-listed** reality unless status changes | Invented listing category (General vs Special vs SFI) |

**HA environment (public context):** HA drug spend fell in 2024–25 — first drop in a decade. Formulary arguments must be **value and appropriate patients**, not “more volume.”

**Hospitality / events while you train KOLs:** meal caps; ≥2/3 science; written FMV contracts for advisors; no branded trinkets.

# Module 5 — Role-play drills (do these out loud)

Run each twice: once as the “compliant PM,” once as the “physician who will not let go.” Stop if you hear a red-line phrase.

### Drill A — Private hematologist, 12 minutes

Physician: uses off-label Pegasys in PV; angry about price; asks ET “while you’re here.”  
Pass if: IFN comparison refused; ET parked to Medical; PSP rules stated without exceptions; one on-label patient profile agreed.

### Drill B — Academic KOL, 20 minutes

Physician: wants six-year EFS unpacked, JAK2 meaning, PARADIGM rumor, Taiwan data.  
Pass if: stats are cited with study names; PARADIGM/RWE deferred to lexicon/Medical; no new local claim.

### Drill C — HA specialist, 12 minutes

Physician: “HU is good enough for public patients; your drug is a private luxury.”  
Pass if: HU titration nuance acknowledged; vs-HU data offered without attacking HA practice; **no listing date**; ask what evidence they would need to see (take back to Medical/Access).

### Drill D — Safety interrupt

Mid-sentence the physician mentions a possible depression signal on interferon.  
Pass if: you stop selling, open ADR/PV path, do not minimize.

### Drill E — Private Pegasys economist (field-real)

Physician: Pegasys ~low-thousands HKD/syringe; BESREMi ~30×; q4w “you said saves money”; Pegasys can extend interval too; asks for a pen because 100 mcg from 500 is waste.  
Pass if: gap acknowledged; PSP rules without HQ price-cut; q4w is PI-stability not cost-win; device answered from HK IFU only; one on-label PV patient named.

### Drill F — Nurse gatekeeper (8 minutes)

Nurse: “Leave brochures, doctor too busy, we use China IFN, call us if…”  
Pass if: approved kit only; injection support if cleared; callback for **PV** patients; no ET date, no congress envelope.

### Drill G — HA “MF is easier” (10 minutes)

Consultant: likes the drug; wants MF/pre-MF because few options; asks ET timing after US FDA; sketches HA free-drug months.  
Pass if: MF/ET stopped; HADF not a date; free-drug parked to Access/Legal; IIT parked to Clinical PM.

### Drill H — First patient sleep (5 minutes)

Physician: first BESREMi patient, mild insomnia, “probably not related.”  
Pass if: PV process first; you do not collude with “not related”; product talk only after the safety path is open.
