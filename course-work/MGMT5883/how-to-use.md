# How to use this draft before you submit

This note is for you. It is not part of the four-page report.

## What the report argues

PharmaEssentia Hong Kong has capable people and a real task. Employee creativity is blocked in two places that matter more than a busy calendar.

1. **Ideas and implementation are split.** Local staff can see useful operating ideas. Claims, price corridors, contracts, and portfolio choices sit at headquarters, and a local explanation waits for global lexicon clearance. Creativity (a new and useful idea) never becomes innovation (implementation).
2. **The only signal is error avoidance.** Delivery, public access, and a clean compliance record are the right floor. When they are the only signal, people treat a novel comment as a compliance risk and stop offering unfinished ideas.

Other obstacles are named in one sentence and then set aside: little slack, narrow roles, and little joint work between commercial and clinical colleagues.

One recommendation each:

1. A quarterly 90-minute idea-to-decision path. Diverge locally, then send headquarters one options-and-recommendation note. Decision rights stay at headquarters. First experiments stay in the operating system around BESREMi.
2. One covered creative assignment per associate each quarter, plus a learning line on the biweekly review. Performance and compliance goals stay. The point is creative self-efficacy on difficult work, through encouragement and a small win.

## What you should add in your own words

The evidence in the draft is structural: decision rights, lexicon clearance, and the goals the office already manages. That is legitimate. A marker will trust it more if you add one sentence you personally saw, one for each obstacle. Do not invent a meeting.

Examples you can write truthfully, in your own words:

- A formulary or promotional line that could not be used until global medical or regulatory review finished.
- A local operating idea that stopped because contract authority or price authority sat above Hong Kong.
- A biweekly review that covers performance and compliance and has no line for what the team learned.

If either addition pushes a section over its page cap, delete the Amabile sentence first.
